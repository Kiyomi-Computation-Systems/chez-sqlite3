# M2 plan: statements and values

Date: 2026-11-14
Status: draft. Settled by the maintainer on 2026-11-14: brief D3 to D6 (§2); Q1 to Q8, asked
before slice 0's review, and R1 and R2, asked after it (§3). Each settled item is the choice the
maintainer picked, with the text of the option picked; the reasons and designs around it are

Brief: [the project brief](2026-11-13-chez-sqlite3-project-brief.md), §6's M2: prepare, bind,
step, columns, reset, finalize, statement tracking, §3f's mapping, §3g's conditions.
Measured: [research/m2-plan/](research/m2-plan/) and, by slice 0's review,
[research/m2-review/](research/m2-review/), each on Guix's 3.53.1 and at the floor, 3.45.1.

M1 opens and closes a connection; M2 runs SQL on it. M3 builds the conveniences (`execute!`,
`query`, transactions) on M2's statement tier, which is public from the start (brief §1).

## 1. What M2 adds

- **Statements:** prepare (`sqlite3_prepare_v3`), finalize, step, reset and clear bindings, each
  owned by its connection's thread (ADR-0010, decision 2) and refused once finalized.
- **Tracking:** a connection holds its live statements, and closing it finalizes them before
  `sqlite3_next_stmt` and `sqlite3_close` (ADR-0012, decision 5). The `statement` counter, there
  since M0, gets its first real counts.
- **Values:** binding Scheme values and reading columns, by brief §3f, with D4 to D6.
- **Conditions:** `&sqlite-error`'s `sql` and `offset` fields filled at last, and the subtypes
  callers catch: `&sqlite-busy`, `&sqlite-constraint`, `&sqlite-read-only` (ADR-0013, decision
  8). Plus D6's condition for TEXT that isn't UTF-8.
- **21 required entry points,** each with its convention row, header check and decoy switch.
- **The public names, `docs/reference.md`, `test-public` and `test-reference`** for each.

**Not in M2:** `execute!`, `query`, `query-row`, the fold, transactions, the settings list, the
busy timeout, interrupt, the change counters and the statement cache (M3); `db_config` and
extensions (M4). No callbacks (brief §4).

## 2. Brief D3 to D6, settled by the maintainer, 2026-11-14

The maintainer's answers, in chat, after the leanings and their reasons were put:

1. **D3: the statement cache waits for M3.**
2. **D4: binding `+nan.0` is refused.** ADR-0015 proposes the rest: the refusal is an assertion
   violation (ADR-0013, decision 7), `+inf.0` and `-inf.0` bind as REAL, and a caller who means
   "missing" passes `sql-null`. SQLite would store NULL and answer `SQLITE_OK` (brief, evidence).
3. **D5: binding `#t` or `#f` is refused; callers pass 1 or 0.** The reasons put to the
   maintainer, which ADR-0015 records as proposed: a read gives back 0, which is true in Scheme;
   `#f` also means "not found" (`assq`, `member`), so accepting it would store 0 for "absent"; a
   refusal can be loosened later, and an acceptance can't be withdrawn.
4. **D6: TEXT that isn't valid UTF-8 raises a named condition naming the column, and the column
   can be read as bytes instead,** the recommendation, as Python's `sqlite3` does. Chez's
   `utf8->string` replaces bad bytes with U+FFFD silently (brief, evidence), so M2 decodes with a
   strict check of its own, whose cost slice 1 measures.

## 3. Q1 to Q8, and R1 and R2, settled by the maintainer, 2026-11-14

Each was put to the maintainer in a picker that listed its alternatives, the recommendation
first, and the maintainer picked the recommendation each time. Q1 to Q8 came before slice 0's
review; R1 and R2 came from it. Each item quotes what the option picked said; anything more is
proposed in §4 and the records.

- **Q1. Short names,** such as `prepare-statement`, `bind!`, `step!`, `reset!` and
  `column-value`, not prefixed ones such as `statement-bind!`. The surface below, in those names,
  is proposed (ADR-0014 and ADR-0015).
- **Q2. `step!` answers `#t` when a row is ready and `#f` when the statement has finished,** not
  the symbols `row` and `done`.
- **Q3. SQL holding more than one statement is refused,** an assertion violation, and empty SQL or
  only a comment is refused too. M3's `execute!` may take a script; a statement never does.
  Declined: ignoring the rest, and handing it back.
- **Q4. `step!` on a done or failed statement is an assertion violation until `reset!`,** so
  nothing reruns unasked and every SQLite build behaves alike. SQLite would run the statement again
  (measured: one `insert` stepped twice made two rows), except on a build with
  `SQLITE_OMIT_AUTORESET`, which answers `SQLITE_MISUSE`.
- **Q5. A finalized statement raises `&sqlite-closed`,** the type a closed connection raises, its
  `&who` naming the procedure called, and a second `finalize-statement!` does nothing. Not a new
  `&sqlite-finalized`.
- **Q6. `&sqlite-error`'s `offset` counts characters,** an index into the `sql` field's string, so
  it points at the token a caller sees. SQLite counts bytes (measured: 13 for a token at character
  12, after an `é`).
- **Q7. `sql-null` is one value,** compared with `eq?` or tested with `sql-null?`. Not a
  procedure, `(sql-null)`.
- **Q8. D6's condition is `&sqlite-invalid-text`,** its own type under `&chez-sqlite3-error`, with
  the column's index and the byte offset of the first bad byte. Not a `&sqlite-error`, since SQLite
  answered `SQLITE_OK`.
- **R1. `close-database!` finalizes every statement, closes the connection, then raises the first
  failure a finalize reported.** The connection ends closed, and the error tells the caller, for
  example, that a commit was lost. If `sqlite3_close` itself then fails, the connection stays open
  and counted, as in M1. The review measured why it is needed: an `insert … returning` stepped once
  commits at its finalize, and there answered BUSY and was rolled back. Declined: raising before
  the close, leaving the connection open.
- **R2. Q3's tail is scanned in Scheme:** a pure function accepts only whitespace, `--` and
  `/* */` comments, and semicolons, and anything else is refused. Nothing the caller didn't ask for
  is prepared. It is tested against SQLite's own prepare as the oracle, over the whole complement.
  The review measured why: merely preparing a PRAGMA changes the connection. Declined: preparing
  the tail and recording the side effect.

The surface, proposed in Q1's names:

```scheme
;; prepare-statement : Database String -> Statement
;; finalize-statement! : Statement -> Void
;; call-with-statement : Database String (Statement -> A ...) -> A ...
;; statement? : Any -> Boolean
;; statement-sql : Statement -> String
;; parameter-count : Statement -> Natural
;; bind! : Statement (U Positive-Integer String) Value -> Void   ; 1-based, or ":name" with its sigil
;; bind-all! : Statement Value ... -> Void                       ; every parameter, 1 to n, in order
;; clear-bindings! : Statement -> Void
;; step! : Statement -> Boolean                                  ; Q2: #t a row, #f done
;; reset! : Statement -> Void
;; column-count : Statement -> Natural
;; column-name : Statement Natural -> String                     ; 0-based, as SQLite's columns are
;; column-value : Statement Natural -> Value
;; column-bytevector : Statement Natural -> (U Bytevector SqlNull) ; TEXT or BLOB as its bytes (D6)
;; sql-null : SqlNull                                            ; Q7: a value
;; sql-null? : Any -> Boolean
;;
;; Value = (U Integer Flonum String Bytevector SqlNull), the Integer exact and within 64 bits

(call-with-statement db "insert into t (id, name) values (?, :name)"
  (lambda (st)
    (for-each (lambda (row)
                (bind! st 1 (car row))
                (bind! st ":name" (cdr row))
                (step! st)
                (reset! st))
              rows)))
```

`call-with-statement` mirrors `call-with-database` (ADR-0012, decision 8). A whole-row reader
waits for M3's `query`.

## 4. Decisions that bind every slice

Each is written into the record named, in slice 0, proposed until the maintainer accepts it, except
where §2 or §3 settled it.

### 4.1 A statement is an integer handle in a sealed record, tracked by its connection (ADR-0014, new)

- **As a connection is** (ADR-0012, decision 2): the `sqlite3_stmt*` as an integer in an opaque,
  sealed `statement` record, its accessor private. SQLite links every statement from its
  connection, so Valgrind sees one never finalized only through that connection; the counters see
  it alone. The record holds its database, the SQL it was given, and its state (§4.2).
- **The connection holds its live statements, strongly.** Prepare adds one, finalize removes it,
  and `close-database!` finalizes each, closes, then raises a finalize's failure (R1). A statement
  `sqlite3_next_stmt` finds that the connection doesn't hold is still a defect, refused as M1
  refuses it.
- **The checks, in order, before anything reaches C:** a statement; on its connection's owning
  thread, else `&sqlite-wrong-thread`; not finalized, else `&sqlite-closed` (Q5). Then the state.
  `finalize-statement!` makes the first two alone, as `close-database!` does, so a second does
  nothing (Q5).
- **Reset and finalize can fail on their own, and lose data.** They repeat a failed step's code,
  which isn't raised again; but in any other state an answer other than `SQLITE_OK` is a new
  failure, such as an `insert … returning` whose commit answered BUSY and was rolled back
  (measured), and raises. Finalize frees the statement whatever it answers (measured), so it counts
  down first and raises after.
- **Prepare, step, reset and finalize are `__collect_safe`.** Reset and finalize joined after the
  review measured each waiting 1502 ms in the busy handler. Each runs inside its verb's critical
  section, so other threads collect while it blocks; this thread's interrupts wait for it, and M3's
  interrupt is how another thread stops a long one.
- **Anything raised is raised after the critical section,** as M1's `open-connection` and
  `close-connection` do, so a caller's `with-exception-handler` handler never runs with interrupts
  disabled (measured: inside, it does; a `guard` clause unwinds first). `call-with-open-handle`
  stays the one accessor of a connection's handle, and every statement verb that needs it,
  `prepare-statement` first, goes through it. M1's `call-with-open-handle` and
  `call-with-database`'s open raise inside their sections today; M1's closing review fixes both
  (ADR-0012, amended), and slice 3 builds on its contract.
- **Closing finalizes oldest first.** Of two statements stepped once under a reader, only the last
  finalize commits, and only it failed (measured), so R1's "first failure" is the one failure the
  case reaches.
- **The tail scan accepts SQLite's own whitespace and comments, exactly** (ADR-0014, decision 4):
  space, `\t`, `\n`, `\f` and `\r`; `--` to the end of the line; `/*` with at least one byte after
  it; semicolons. `\v`, U+0085, U+00A0 and a final `/*` are errors to SQLite (measured).
- **A refused SQL's first statement has still been prepared,** so its prepare-time effects stand:
  `pragma cache_size = 7; select 1` is refused, and `cache_size` is 7 (measured). Slice 3 holds it
  by a row, and the docs say so.

### 4.2 A statement's state lives in Scheme, so SQLite's undefined cases never reach C (ADR-0014)

SQLite leaves a column read without a row, or out of range, undefined, answers `MISUSE` to a bind
while a row is ready, and lets `sqlite3_clear_bindings` free what a ready row still reads (all
measured but the first, which is documented). So each record keeps a state, and every verb is
checked against it first:

| State | Reached by | Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| `ready` | prepare, `reset!` | `bind!`, `bind-all!`, `clear-bindings!`, `step!`, `reset!` |
| `row` | `step!` answering a row | column reads, `step!`, `reset!` |
| `done` | `step!` answering done | `reset!` |
| `failed` | a step answering an error code | `reset!` |
| finalized | `finalize-statement!`, or its connection's close | nothing |

`finalize-statement!` is allowed in every state, and does nothing on a finalized statement.
`column-count`, `column-name`, `statement-sql` and `parameter-count` work in every state but
finalized. A refusal before C leaves the state as it was. A verb not allowed is an assertion
violation naming the state. A parameter outside
`parameter-count` is an assertion violation before C, and so is a column outside the column count,
read in the same critical section and never cached: it changes when another connection alters the
table (measured).

### 4.3 Binding (ADR-0015, new; brief §3f, D4, D5)

| Scheme value | Binds as | Refused |
|---|---|---|
| exact integer | `sqlite3_bind_int64` | outside −2⁶³ to 2⁶³−1 |
| flonum | `sqlite3_bind_double` | `+nan.0` (D4) |
| string | `sqlite3_bind_text64`, UTF-8, its byte length | — |
| bytevector | `sqlite3_bind_blob64`, its length | — |
| `sql-null` | `sqlite3_bind_null` | — |
| `#t`, `#f` (D5), an exact non-integer, a complex, anything else | — | an assertion violation, naming the value's kind |

- **The integer range is our check:** Chez's `integer-64` wraps 2⁶³ to −2⁶³ and 2⁶⁴−1 to −1
  silently (measured). The refused boundary rows are 2⁶³, 2⁶⁴−1 and −2⁶³−1, each asserting the
  rule's symbol.
- **A bind SQLite refuses,** such as TOOBIG over a length limit, raises `&sqlite-error`, and leaves
  the parameter NULL (measured). `bind-all!` checks every value before binding any, but a refusal
  of SQLite's midway leaves the ones before it bound.
- **Bound text and blobs are counted copies,** in a foreign block freed after the call, passed with
  `SQLITE_TRANSIENT` so SQLite copies them (ADR-0011, decision 2). Since the length is given, a
  bound string keeps a U+0000. ADR-0011 is amended: its "a string holding U+0000 is refused"
  covers strings SQLite reads to a NUL, not counted values.
- **An empty string or bytevector still passes a non-NULL pointer.** SQLite binds NULL for a NULL
  pointer (measured with length 0; its documentation says the length is then ignored).
- **`-0.0` binds as REAL and comes back `0.0` from a REAL column** (measured). Recorded, not
  refused.

### 4.4 Columns (ADR-0015; brief §3f, D6)

- **The value's type comes from `sqlite3_column_type`,** never from the declared type: INTEGER
  gives an exact integer, FLOAT a flonum, TEXT a string, BLOB a bytevector, NULL `sql-null`. A
  column declared `BOOLEAN` reads back 1 and 0.
- **TEXT is read through `sqlite3_column_text` and BLOB through `sqlite3_column_blob`,** each then
  `sqlite3_column_bytes`, and copied out by our code (ADR-0011, decision 4); `column-bytevector`
  reads TEXT the same way. `sqlite3_column_blob` answers NULL for an empty TEXT (measured). An
  embedded NUL survives.
- **A NULL pointer is told from running out of memory** by `sqlite3_errcode`, read at once: only
  `SQLITE_NOMEM` raises. `sqlite3_column_name` answering NULL sets no code (the floor's source), so
  it raises `nomem` itself, with `sqlite3_errstr`'s message. Neither path can be caused on a real
  library, so both are one pure reader tested with stand-ins.
- **`column-name` decodes strictly too,** raising `&sqlite-invalid-text` with the column's index.
- **TEXT is decoded strictly** (D6), by Unicode's table of well-formed sequences. The offset is
  where the first maximal ill-formed subpart starts (Unicode §3.9), as Python reports it.
- **A UTF-16 database converts its text to UTF-8 on the way out** (measured), and some invalid
  UTF-16 converts to valid UTF-8 for another character. The strict check guards UTF-8 databases,
  and `docs/reference.md` says so.

### 4.5 Strings into SQLite (ADR-0011, amended)

- **SQL text holding U+0000 is refused** before anything is allocated, as a filename is. A NUL
  ends SQLite's parse even with the byte length given (measured).
- **A parameter name is a C string,** for `sqlite3_bind_parameter_index`, and is refused the same
  way. A name no parameter has is an assertion violation.

### 4.6 Errors (ADR-0013, amended)

- **Prepare's errors carry the SQL and the offset** (Q6's unit), `#f` when SQLite gives −1, as it
  does for an error at the end of the SQL (measured).
- **Every other `&sqlite-error` a statement raises carries its statement's SQL and no offset:**
  step's, reset's and finalize's own, a refused bind's, a column read's and `column-name`'s out of
  memory, and the finalize failure `close-database!` raises (ADR-0013, amended).
- **A failed step answers its extended code itself** (measured, 2067), so the code comes from the
  step, and the message from `sqlite3_errmsg` on the owning thread straight after.
- **The subtypes are classed by primary code** (decision 8): `busy` and `locked` give
  `&sqlite-busy`, `constraint` gives `&sqlite-constraint`, `readonly` gives `&sqlite-read-only`.
  Each is a `&sqlite-error` with the same fields. The classing is a pure function, tested over
  every primary code, so a code that should give no subtype is checked too.

### 4.7 The required entry points, and what they cost the gate (ADR-0002, ADR-0005, amended)

- **Required in M2:** `sqlite3_prepare_v3`, `sqlite3_finalize`, `sqlite3_step`, `sqlite3_reset`,
  `sqlite3_clear_bindings`, `sqlite3_bind_parameter_count`, `sqlite3_bind_parameter_index`,
  `sqlite3_bind_null`, `sqlite3_bind_int64`, `sqlite3_bind_double`, `sqlite3_bind_text64`,
  `sqlite3_bind_blob64`, `sqlite3_column_count`, `sqlite3_column_name`, `sqlite3_column_type`,
  `sqlite3_column_int64`, `sqlite3_column_double`, `sqlite3_column_text`, `sqlite3_column_blob`,
  `sqlite3_column_bytes` and `sqlite3_error_offset`: 21, all present at the floor. Four are
  `__collect_safe`: prepare, step, reset and finalize (§4.1).
- **Each needs its decoy switch, and each switch is a gated child.** `test-library-loading` takes
  308 s of `MEMORY_TIMEOUT`'s 600 on CI, about 5.6 s per child (ADR-0005, amended). 21 more is
  about 120 s, near 430 s, by arithmetic, with M3 and M4 still to add theirs. Slice 2 measures it
  on CI; past 450 s, three quarters of the limit, the suite is split in two.

### 4.8 What M2 plants

Each becomes a test, watched failing against a planted copy:
- **the assessment's defects** (ADR-0009): prepare's out-parameter block never freed, and a close
  that stops after finalizing the first statement, Thunderchez's `or` over return codes, where 0
  is true;
- **guards of our own:** a reset, finalize or close that drops a failure of its own (R1);
  `clear-bindings!` let through while a row is ready, planted with a value bigger than SQLite's
  lookaside slots so Valgrind sees the free (ADR-0005, amended); `sqlite3_bind_int64` or
  `sqlite3_column_int64` bound as `int`; booleans guessed from a declared type; and parameters
  numbered from 0. The assessment lists the last two among what Thunderchez does well; this
  project rejects them.

## 5. Order of work: M2's slices

Each slice is one PR, with its own mutation log, and ends with an Opus adversarial review. A check
isn't finished until it has been watched failing. Each slice's brief quotes the AGENTS.md rules
that bind what it builds (M0's closing review).

0. **The plan and the records.** This plan; ADR-0014, statements; ADR-0015, values; amendments to
   ADR-0002 (the entry points), ADR-0005 (statements, lookaside, the decoy's cost), ADR-0011
   (counted values, SQL text, the four `__collect_safe` calls), ADR-0012 (tracking, R1), ADR-0013
   (the subtypes, `offset`, Q8's type, the narrowed U+0000 rule), the brief (§5's D3 to D6) and
   the global constraints. The probes, on Guix's 3.53.1 and at the floor.
1. **The pure core,** on synthetic inputs, no foreign code:
   - the bind plan for a value (§4.3, with D4 and D5, and the integer boundary rows);
   - the strict UTF-8 decoder over bytevectors, tested exhaustively on 1- and 2-byte inputs, at the
     3- and 4-byte forms' range edges and at every position, with its offsets, and its cost
     measured against `utf8->string`;
   - the tail scanner (R2), whose oracle, SQLite's prepare, joins in slice 3;
   - the state table (§4.2);
   - the subtype classing (§4.6);
   - byte offsets into character indexes (Q6);
   - the NULL-pointer reader of §4.4, `sqlite3_column_name`'s NULL among it, on stand-ins.
2. **The bindings.** §4.7's entry points through `bind-required`; the constants (`SQLITE_ROW`,
   `SQLITE_DONE`, the five type codes, `SQLITE_UTF8`, `SQLITE_TRANSIENT`) checked against
   `sqlite3.h`; the convention rows; `test-native` against the oracle; a decoy switch per entry
   point, and their cost measured on CI.
3. **Statements.** Prepare, finalize, `call-with-statement`, step, reset; tracking and the close
   that finalizes (R1); the thread and state checks; `sql` and `offset`; the subtypes, each reached
   by real SQL (a constraint, a read-only database, a lock held by a second connection); the tail
   scanner against SQLite's prepare; the four `__collect_safe` calls held by behaviour, each made
   to block by a busy timeout and a second connection's lock, each row asserting that another
   thread's collection finished while the call was still blocked, the prepare's on a fresh
   connection (ADR-0011, amended); every statement verb raising after its section, checked
   from a `with-exception-handler` handler; a refused SQL whose first statement's prepare-time
   effect stands;
   reset's and finalize's own failures,
   through an `insert … returning` under a reader; the leaks plant through `prepare-statement`;
   §4.8's lifecycle plants; whether `call-with-statement` survives an engine's preemption,
   measured; the public names and their docs.
4. **Values.** `bind!`, `bind-all!`, `clear-bindings!`, the column reads and `sql-null`, end to
   end: every row of §4.3 and §4.4 on a real library, D4 to D6 among them, `column-name`'s strict
   decode, a refused bind through `sqlite3_limit` with a value over 30 bytes (3.53.1 clamps the
   limit to at least 30), the suite binding `sqlite3_limit` itself, and §4.8's value plants. The
   public names and their docs.

Then the maintainer's hand run on the Mac, and M2's closing review.
