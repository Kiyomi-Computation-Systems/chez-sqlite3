# M1 plan: loading

Date: 2026-11-13
Status: approved in parts, the rest proposed with slice 0's records. The maintainer settled four
questions on 2026-11-13 (§2), and approved the slicing (§5), the open surface and the handle as an
integer in a sealed record (§3.1) the same day. On 2026-11-14 the maintainer settled four more
(§2): the condition root, the closed type, a library that keeps no memory count, and
`call-with-database`'s shape. The rest of §3 is proposed in slice 0's records, ADR-0010 to
ADR-0013, and its amendments to ADR-0002, ADR-0005, ADR-0009 and the brief, for the maintainer's
acceptance with slice 0's PR.
Source: chez-curl at `79c6ced` (`main`), surveyed read-only by a Sonnet agent and spot-checked
here. Every chez-curl path below is under `src/curl/private/` or `tests/`.
Brief: [the project brief](2026-11-13-chez-sqlite3-project-brief.md), §6's M1. Harness plan:
[§4 and §5](2026-11-13-harness-plan.md), the pieces that wait for library code.

M1 finds, loads and checks libsqlite3, and opens and closes a connection. Nothing runs SQL: that
is M2. chez-curl built its loading layer in its own M0, so this plan copies from chez-curl's code
and records, not from a chez-curl plan.

## 1. What M1 adds

- **Discovery and the load** (ADR-0002, ADR-0003). The override, else one candidate list per world,
  loaded once by absolute path. Then the floor, then `sqlite3_threadsafe`, then every required
  entry point, each refused by a reason symbol. The first failure is kept for the life of the
  process.
- **The report:** path, source, `sqlite3_libversion`, its number and `sqlite3_sourceid`
  (ADR-0002, amended). An application asks for compile options through an exported query, which
  calls `sqlite3_compileoption_used` (brief §2.6).
- **Connections:** `open-database`, `close-database!` and `call-with-database` (brief §3e), owned by
  the thread that opened them (§3c). They open with `SQLITE_OPEN_FULLMUTEX` unless asked otherwise
  (§2), and always with `SQLITE_OPEN_EXRESCODE`.
- **Conditions:** the condition types loading and opening raise (§3g). Statement errors and their
  subtypes are M2's.
- **The checks of the FFI:** `test-constants` (a C program compiled against `sqlite3.h`), a
  convention suite, and `check-seam`.
- **The gate's other two parts** (ADR-0005): `sqlite3_memory_used` returns to its baseline, and
  `sqlite3_next_stmt` finds no untracked statement at a close (§3.7). Plus a leaks plant through the first real
  constructor, and Valgrind over every child process that reaches native code.
- **`make build`, `make dev`, `check-dev`,** the decoy libsqlite3 and the refusal probe.
- **`docs/reference.md`, `test-reference` and `test-docs`,** from the first public export on.
- **CI's `floor` and `current` lanes** (harness plan §5).
- **Brief D7,** measured on the maintainer's Mac.

**Not in M1:** statements, binding and values (M2); the settings list, the busy timeout and
interrupt (M3); `db_config` and extension loading (M4).

## 2. Settled by the maintainer, 2026-11-13

1. **The settings list moves to M3.** Brief §2.7 runs PRAGMAs through the statement path, and a
   checked setting reads its row back: `journal_mode=wal` on a `:memory:` database answers
   `memory`, while every call succeeds ([research/m1-plan/](research/m1-plan/)). M2 builds that
   path; M3 builds the list beside the busy timeout. The brief's §6 is amended.
2. **Brief D2: `SQLITE_OPEN_FULLMUTEX` by default, configurable per connection.** One open option
   selects `SQLITE_OPEN_NOMUTEX`. The thread check of §3c holds either way.
3. **A `THREADSAFE=0` libsqlite3 is refused** with a new reason, `not-threadsafe`, checked after the
   floor. Such a build has no mutexes, so even two connections on two threads are unsafe.
   ADR-0002's open question is closed by amendment.
4. **Brief D7 is measured on the maintainer's one Mac,** on its current macOS. macOS 14 and 15
   stay unmeasured and are recorded so. Measured the same day, macOS 26.7.1 on arm64
   ([research/mac-d7/](research/mac-d7/)): Apple's SQLite is 3.51.0, above the floor, and loads
   by path from the dyld cache with no file on disk, so macOS keeps its one candidate. It is
   `THREADSAFE=2`, `DEFAULT_MEMSTATUS=0` and `OMIT_LOAD_EXTENSION`. Homebrew's is 3.54.1, with
   extension loading.

Settled by the maintainer, 2026-11-14:

5. **The condition hierarchy has a fieldless root,** `&chez-sqlite3-error`. `&sqlite-error`, the
   base that carries SQLite's codes, `&sqlite-library-unavailable`, `&sqlite-wrong-thread` and
   `&sqlite-closed` sit under it (ADR-0013).
6. **A verb on a closed connection raises its own type,** `&sqlite-closed`. A second
   `close-database!` does nothing (ADR-0012).
7. **On a library that keeps no memory count,** such as Apple's, a suite reports the memory check
   unavailable, by name, and passes on its other checks. The positive control of §3.6 detects it
   (ADR-0005, amended).
8. **`call-with-database` takes the procedure last:** `(call-with-database "app.db" 'read-only
   (lambda (db) …))` (ADR-0012).

## 3. Decisions that bind every slice

Each is written into the record named, in slice 0, and is proposed there unless §2 records the
maintainer's choice.

### 3.1 A connection's handle is an integer, inside a record (ADR-0012; approved 2026-11-13)

M0 slice 3 measured both ways ([research/memory-gate/](research/memory-gate/)). A handle held as an
integer is definitely lost at exit when the connection was never closed, even while a variable
still holds it; in an ftype pointer, the same leak is still reachable and the gate passes. Leak
checking runs at exit, and a suite must close everything before it exits, so the integer form makes
Valgrind a second witness to every connection left open, beside the counters. A connection closed
before exit is freed, so the integer adds no failure our rules don't already make. chez-curl held
its blocks in ftype pointers and relied on its counters alone (chez-curl ADR-0014).

An opaque, sealed record holds the integer, and its accessor stays private, so no caller can forge
or reuse one. That takes over the check an ftype pointer typed `(* sqlite3)` would give at the FFI
boundary: the accessor raises on anything that isn't a database, where `uptr` takes any integer.

The probe made two calls with its handle. Whether a stale raw copy of the address survives a real
library's many calls, and makes the leak "still reachable" again, is not known. Slice 4's leaks
plant settles it: it opens through `open-database`, drops the record, and is watched failing under
Valgrind as well as through the counters. If Valgrind can't see it, that is recorded, and the
counters still hold.

### 3.2 Nothing reaches libsqlite3 from Scheme memory (ADR-0011)

Every string passed to libsqlite3, the filename among them, is copied into a
counted foreign block, NUL-terminated, and freed after the call. A string holding a NUL is refused
before anything is allocated. `__collect_safe` calls take only such copies and integers (brief
§3d). In M1 the calls that can block are `sqlite3_close` and `sqlite3_open_v2`, which creates the
file before any statement runs ([research/m1-plan/](research/m1-plan/)).

### 3.3 The load is ordered definitions, and every step is in `load-step` (ADR-0002)

As in chez-curl's `native.sls`: step 0 re-raises a recorded failure; then choose, load, version,
check, bind. Nothing binds lazily. Every required entry point is checked with `foreign-entry?`
before it is bound. A guard can't catch a raise from an imported library's body, so probes `eval`
their first reference (AGENTS.md).

### 3.4 Every refusal is asserted by its reason, and every outcome by its whole output

Tests assert the reason symbol and every field of the outcome, the whole printed `(key . value)`
lines of a probe among them, and never a message or remedy text (ADR-0007, ADR-0013). A child process that reaches native code prints its counts, and its suite asserts them
zero.

### 3.5 A trimmed copy lists what each dropped piece protected

M0's three slices each lost a protection by trimming a copy (`.plans/traps.md`). So each slice's
brief carries a table for every piece it copies: what is kept, what is dropped, what each dropped
piece protected, and where that protection now lives, or why nothing here needs it. The reason is
checked against every rule the copy keeps before the brief goes out.

### 3.6 A check of SQLite's own counters has a positive control

`sqlite3_memory_used` returns 0 on a build compiled with `SQLITE_DEFAULT_MEMSTATUS=0`, so a check
that it returns to its baseline would pass without testing anything. The check must first see the
count rise while a connection is open. Neither Linux build recorded so far lists
`DEFAULT_MEMSTATUS` among its options, so both keep the default, 1. **Apple's does:**
`DEFAULT_MEMSTATUS=0`, and its count stays 0 while a database is open
([research/mac-d7/](research/mac-d7/)). There a suite reports the check unavailable, by name, and
passes on its other checks (§2, decision 7). A silent pass is ruled out. The Mac's hand runs reach
Apple's library by default.

### 3.7 The testing library never loads libsqlite3 (M0's closing review, finding 3)

`(chez-sqlite3-testing)` and the counters it imports never load libsqlite3
(`src/chez-sqlite3/private/counters.sls`'s header). Several things depend on that:
- `check-suites`, which runs before every `make test`;
- `check-test-fails`' copy of the tree, which holds only `require-threaded` and the counters;
- the launcher's tests, which run `make test` under `env -i`, so a Guix-world search would find
  nothing;
- the seven children `test-chez-sqlite3-testing` starts, which would become children that reach
  native code.

So SQLite's two checks don't go into `suite-exit-status`, where the harness plan's §3 first put
them. PR #6 amended that §3 and ADR-0005 to follow decision 5:
- **`sqlite3_next_stmt` runs in `close-database!`,** before `sqlite3_close`, as ADR-0005 decision
  5 and brief §2.4 say. A connection is already closed by the time a suite exits.
- **`sqlite3_memory_used` is asked only in a process that loaded the library.** The leaning: the
  load registers its exit check with the counters, which `suite-exit-status` already reads, so a
  suite that never loaded libsqlite3 has nothing to ask. Settled at slice 4's start, and
  recorded in ADR-0005.

It rested on a comment, and `check-seam` reads `src/` alone, so it can't hold
`tests/chez-sqlite3-testing.sls`. Slice 4's `test-never-loads` holds it, and passed before the exit
check's registration arrived (ADR-0005, amended).

### 3.8 `check-ci` is loosened per job, never for the whole file (M0's closing review, finding 4)

Slices 3 and 5 add jobs, `env:` keys and cache steps that `check-ci` refuses today. That
fails loudly, which is right. The risk is in the loosening:
- **`main` is the only job that tests discovery,** since the container always sets the override.
  `CHEZ_SQLITE3_LIBRARY` stays banned in `main` when the `floor` and `current` jobs gain it.
- **`CC`, `C_INCLUDE_PATH`, `CPATH`, `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` and `LD_PRELOAD` join the banned names**
  before any job gains an `env:` key beyond `CHEZ`. Each can steer `test-constants` or the load.
- **A change to the workflow changes `tests/check-ci.sh` and its suite with it** (AGENTS.md), with
  each new rule watched failing.

### 3.9 What else M1 inherits from M0 (M0's closing review)

- **No build output under `src/`.** `test-require-threaded` refuses any `.so` there, so `make build`
  and anything compiled for a test write under `build/`.
- **`check-seam` reads `src/` alone.** `(chez-sqlite3-testing)` and `test-counters` import
  `(chez-sqlite3 private counters)`, and the plants and probes call `foreign-alloc`.
- **Children under Valgrind keep a log per process** (`%p`), each one read by the evidence rules.
  The gate reads one log's first `Command:` today, so a child's summary written into the parent's
  log would never be read. Slice 3 measured `--trace-children` and chose instead to run each child
  under the gate's own Valgrind (ADR-0005, amended): traced, Chez's static bash fails every child.
- **`check-test-fails`' leaks plant raises the count itself,** and its copy holds two libraries.
  Slice 4's leaks plant through `open-database` replaces it.
- **The gate's "no suppression" was measured in the container only** (Valgrind 3.27, SQLite
  3.53.1). CI's memory job (Valgrind 3.22, Ubuntu's 3.45.1) has never run a suite that loads
  libsqlite3. The first one that does is that measurement, and its slice records it.

## 5. Order of work: M1's slices

Each slice is one PR, with its own mutation log in the newer format, and ends with an Opus
adversarial review. A check isn't finished until it has been watched failing.

0. **The plan and the records.** This plan; ADR-0010 to ADR-0013; amendments to ADR-0002
   (`not-threadsafe`, the Mac), ADR-0005 (no memory count), ADR-0009 (the seam) and the brief
   (§2, §5, §6); M0's closing-review close-out; and the D7 probe, which the maintainer ran on the
   Mac.
   - **ADR-0010,** a connection belongs to the thread that opened it (brief §3c), and the mutex mode
     (D2).
   - **ADR-0011,** calls that can block are `__collect_safe`, and nothing passed to libsqlite3 lives
     in Scheme memory (brief §3d).
   - **ADR-0012,** closing is explicit, with no guardians, and the handle is an integer in a record
     (brief §3e).
   - **ADR-0013,** errors are condition types (brief §3g): the root, the base `&sqlite-error`, codes
     as symbols, and which types M1 and M2 each build.
1. **The pure core.** Discovery, `load-state`, the loading conditions, the report record and the
   checks, with their suites, all on synthetic inputs. No foreign code. macOS keeps its one
   candidate, `/usr/lib/libsqlite3.dylib` (D7, measured).
2. **The load and the checks of the FFI.** The native library's body, from choosing to binding; its
   suite against a C oracle that reads the same library; `test-constants`; the convention suite;
   `check-seam`; and `make build`.
3. **Refusals end to end.** The decoy, the refusal probe and `test-library-loading`; `make dev` and
   `check-dev`; and Valgrind over the probes:
   - **Measured first:** whether the gate follows children (`--trace-children=yes`, a log per
     process) or each child runs under the gate's own Valgrind with its own log. ADR-0005 is
     amended with the choice.
   - **CI:** the `main` job runs `make build` with the override unset, as the install check ADR-0002
     asks for, and must find the runner's library through discovery. `check-ci` and its suite change
     with the workflow.
4. **The public library and connections.**
   - **The first public exports** of `(chez-sqlite3)`: the report, version, compile options,
     `open-database`, `close-database!`, `call-with-database` and the conditions.
   - **Connections:** thread ownership, the open options (mutex mode among them), and the
     connection counter.
   - **The gate:** a leaks plant through `open-database`; `sqlite3_next_stmt` in
     `close-database!`; and `sqlite3_memory_used` at the exit of a suite that loaded the library
     (§3.7). Measured first, on CI's runner: whether 3.45.1's `sqlite3_memory_used` returns to
     its pre-open value after a full open and close, or needs a baseline taken after the first
     cycle. 3.53.1 and 3.54.1 returned to it (ADR-0005, amended).
   - **The docs:** `docs/reference.md`, with `test-reference` and `test-docs`.
5. **The `floor` and `current` lanes.** `build-sqlite.sh`, cached like Chez, at 3.45.1 and at a
   pinned current release, with fixed `-DSQLITE_*` flags. Each lane asserts the version and
   compile options it built before its suites run, and loads it through the override with its own
   header on `C_INCLUDE_PATH`. `check-ci` and its suite change with the workflow. First,
   `check-floors` extends to `scripts/ci/`, which it doesn't read today, so the script that
   fetches the amalgamation can't write the floor's number unseen (ADR-0008, amended at M0's
   closing review).

**The open surface** (slice 4), approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-13. ADR-0012 records it:

```scheme
;; open-database : String Symbol ... -> Database
;; Options: read-only | read-write (the default, with create), create, uri, memory, no-follow,
;; no-mutex. A contradiction (read-only with create) or an unknown option is refused.
(open-database "app.db")
(open-database "file:app.db?mode=ro" 'uri 'read-only)
(open-database "app.db" 'no-mutex)

;; call-with-database : String Symbol ... (Database -> Any) -> Any   (2026-11-14)
(call-with-database "app.db" 'read-only
  (lambda (db) …))
```

ADR-0012 spells out the options: `'read-write` alone doesn't create the file, and an option given
twice is refused too.
