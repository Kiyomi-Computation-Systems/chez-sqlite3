## 1. What M3 adds

- **The conveniences:** `execute!`, `query`, `query-row`, `query-fold`, and a script runner,
  `execute-script!` (Q4), each running its SQL through M2's checked prepare (ADR-0016).
- **Transactions:** `with-transaction`, with nested levels as savepoints, and rollback on a raise
  (ADR-0017).
- **The counters:** `changes`, `total-changes` and `last-insert-rowid`, and `in-transaction?`
  (ADR-0016).
- **The settings list:** checked per-connection settings, run at every open (brief §2.7;
  ADR-0018).
- **Interrupt:** `interrupt-database!`, the one verb another thread may call, designed with
  `close-database!` (ADR-0010, decision 1; ADR-0019).
- **The busy timeout,** as a setting, and what it promises (ADR-0019).
- **The statement cache** (brief D3; ADR-0020), 32 statements a connection by default, set by
  the `statement-cache` setting, 0 turning it off (the maintainer's Q11).
- **The contention suite:** two connections in WAL mode under a busy timeout.
- **Five required entry points,** each with its convention row, header check and decoy switch,
  their rows continuing the alternation of entry points between `test-library-loading-decoys`
  and `test-library-loading-real`, the halves M2 split the suite into in PR #19 (ADR-0005,
  amended). M3 slice 2 split the halves in three, with `test-library-loading-missing`, dealing
  entry points by a pattern of five (ADR-0005's amendment "the halves split in three").
- **The public names, `docs/reference.md`, `test-public` and `test-reference`** for each.

**Not in M3:** `db_config` and extensions (M4). No callbacks (brief §4): no busy handler, progress
handler or `sqlite3_exec` callback. No backup, blob I/O or unlock-notify (brief §4).

## 2. Given by M2, settled

The maintainer's, or M2's records', before this plan. None is a question here.

1. **D3, the statement cache, is M3's** (brief §5).
2. **Nothing raises inside a critical section** (AGENTS.md; ADR-0012, amended), **and every value
   fits its C type before the section** (ADR-0011's slice 2 amendment).
3. **An interrupt doesn't end a lock wait** (concurrency I6; ADR-0014's slice 3 amendment).
4. **A column read that runs out of memory moves its statement to `failed` and is raised once.**
   Inside a transaction, the reset or finalize after it ends the transaction, and raises nothing
   (single txn-special G2, G3; ADR-0014's slice 3 amendment, the maintainer's "Keep it: no second
   raise"). So `with-transaction` reads `sqlite3_get_autocommit` to know whether its transaction
   survived.
5. **No callbacks in v1** (brief §4).
6. **One statement per prepare** (the M2 plan's Q3): `prepare-statement` refuses SQL holding none,
   or more than one.
7. **A NULL pointer's code 7 is NOMEM only when the column's type has turned NULL** (ADR-0015's
   slice 4 amendment, PR #19, accepted): a stale 7 from another statement had made empty BLOBs
   raise. M3's reads inherit it through `read-outcome` (§4.1), and nothing in M3 assumes the
   old rule.
8. **M2's re-entry pattern:** a `dynamic-wind`'s before thunks do nothing, so a non-matching
   guard's re-entry goes through (`connection.sls:465–470`, `statement.sls:436`); a handler
   around the procedure records a raise; and a critical winder inside the plain one does the
   finish, its failure raised by the plain one (`statement.sls:444–477`, `connection.sls:478–520`).
   M2's finishes need no flag, being idempotent through their resource, a finalized statement or a
   closed connection. M2's session agrees that `with-transaction` and `query-fold` copy the
   pattern (§4.1, §4.2), and M3 adds flags of its own where its finish isn't idempotent.

- **Q1. The new public names: "Short names"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). `execute!`,
  `execute-script!`, `query`, `query-row`, `query-fold`, `with-transaction`, `changes`,
  `total-changes`, `last-insert-rowid`, `in-transaction?` and `interrupt-database!`, with the
  conditions' `sqlite-interrupt?`, `sqlite-setting-mismatch?` and its three accessors, and
  `sqlite-transaction-ended?`. The counters take the names of SQLite's own SQL functions.
  Declined: the per-connection readers prefixed `database-`, and prefixed `sqlite-`.
- **Q2. What a row is: "A vector"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). A vector of the row's values,
  in column order; the fold calls `(proc acc row)`, as R6RS `fold-left` calls its procedure.
  Declined: a list; an association list by column name, since names can repeat (M2 slice 4's
  research, §4, `research/m2-slice-4/` on PR #19); the columns as the fold's arguments.
- **Q3. `query-row` with no row, or more than one: "#f, or the first"** (the maintainer,
  2026-11-15). `#f` for no row; the first row of several, the rest never read. Declined: a new
  condition for no row; refusing a second row.
- **Q4. `execute!` and scripts: "Two verbs"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). `execute!` runs one
  statement with parameters and answers nothing; `execute-script!` runs a script, with no
  parameters. Declined: `execute!` answering `changes`, stale after a statement SQLite doesn't
  count (single B2, C3, C4); `execute!` taking a script when given no parameters; no script runner
  in v1.
- **Q5. `with-transaction`'s mode: "Mode arg, immediate"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). An
  optional mode, `deferred`, `immediate` or `exclusive`, defaulting to `immediate`. The reason
  given: the busy timeout covers `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and never a deferred transaction's first write
  after a read (concurrency B2, B5, B6, trap 4). Slice 0 narrowed it after the answer: the busy
  timeout covers `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` only while the connection holds no read of its own. Beside its
  own held SELECT, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` answered BUSY at once (slice-0 P12), or 517 in WAL after
  another's commit (slice-0 P9). Q14 settled what follows: `with-transaction` is refused beside a
  held row. Declined: defaulting to `deferred`; no mode
  at all, either way.
- **Q6. A `COMMIT` that fails and leaves the transaction open: "Roll back, raise"** (the
  maintainer, 2026-11-15). Roll back, then raise the commit's failure, so the transaction never
  outlives `with-transaction` (single E13, O7; concurrency B7, B11). Declined: leaving it open;
  retrying the commit.
- **Q7. A body that returns after its transaction has ended: "Raise a new condition"** (the
  maintainer, 2026-11-15). Read autocommit, issue no `COMMIT` or `RELEASE`, and raise
  `&sqlite-transaction-ended` (single L1). Declined: issuing the `COMMIT` and raising SQLite's
  generic `error`; returning the body's answers.
- **Q8. The settings in v1: "Six"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). `busy-timeout`,
  `foreign-keys`, `journal-mode`, `synchronous`, `temp-store` and `trusted-schema`. Declined: eleven,
  and three. Q11's answer adds a seventh setting, the library's own (§4.3); P10 is no longer
  needed.
- **Q9. `journal-mode` on an in-memory database: "Mismatch, raise"** (the maintainer,
  2026-11-15). The read-back differs, so the open closes the connection and raises
  `&sqlite-setting-mismatch` (single P11; concurrency S4). Declined: accepting `memory`; refusing
  the setting for an in-memory open before it opens.
- **Q10. A default busy timeout: "None, SQLite's 0"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). A
  connection keeps SQLite's 0 unless its settings set one (concurrency B1). Declined: a default
  such as 5000 ms.
- **Q11. The statement cache's size: "32, configurable"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15), the
  option "32 by default, and a setting, `statement-cache`, from 0 (off) up". **Not the
  recommendation**, which was 32, fixed. Its consequences are worked out in §4.3 and §4.6, and one
  is Q13. Declined: 32 fixed; 128 fixed; off by default.
- **Q12. An interrupt that lands between statements: "SQLite's way, documented"** (the
  maintainer, 2026-11-15). An interrupt reaches the statement running when it lands, and a
  watchdog repeats it until the work stops (concurrency I2). Declined: a flag the next statement
  verb consumes.

### Q13. An upper bound for the `statement-cache` setting

Q11 made the cache's size a setting, from 0 up. Does it also have a largest value? Each cached
statement holds heap, 1.8 to 2.3 KB for a small SELECT (single K1) and more for a larger one, and
stays a tracked statement until the close. No measurement gives a largest useful size, and
ADR-0020 rejects an unbounded cache for a long-lived connection that runs generated SQL.

- **A. A cap of 1024,** refused above as any value outside a setting's domain is, an assertion
  violation before anything opens (recommended). About 2 MB of heap at single K1's figure for a
  small statement. It can be raised later.
- **B. No cap: any exact natural.** The memory is the caller's to spend; a connection that runs
  generated SQL then holds up to that many statements.
- **C. A smaller cap, 256,** eight times the default.

**Recommendation: A.** A refused value can be accepted later, and an accepted one can't be
withdrawn: the reason given for D5 (ADR-0015, decision 4) and for Q8's recommendation. 1024 is
32 times the default. No measurement says how many distinct statements a program runs hot.
**Evidence:** single K1; ADR-0020, Rejected ("Unbounded").

**Answer: "Cap at 1024"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). A value above 1024 is refused as an
assertion violation before anything opens. Declined: no cap; a cap of 256.

### Q14. A write, or a `with-transaction`, inside `query-fold`'s procedure

Raised by the plan's adversarial review, after Q1 to Q13. `query-fold` holds its statement at a
row while its procedure runs (§4.1). What should a write, or a `with-transaction`, do inside that
procedure on the same connection? Measured on both libraries, alike:

- **beside the connection's own held read,** under another's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and write, its
  `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and an autocommit INSERT answered BUSY at once, where without the held read
  they waited out a 1500 ms busy timeout, in WAL and rollback-journal mode (slice-0 P12);
- in WAL, after another connection's commit, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` there answered 517 at once (slice-0
  P9);
- an interrupt made while the procedure runs sticks behind the fold's row (concurrency I3). Then
  a `with-transaction` there can't prepare its rollback, and its transaction stays open (slice-0
  P7), breaking Q6's promise that the transaction never outlives `with-transaction`;
- with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` taken before the read, writes and savepoints beside it ran at once
  (slice-0 P12): a fold run inside `with-transaction` can write.

- **A. Allow both, and document it:** under contention they fail at once, where without the
  held read they would have waited. Run the fold inside `with-transaction` when its procedure writes. An
  interrupt during the procedure can leave a `with-transaction` there open.
- **B. Refuse `with-transaction` while its connection holds a row,** a fold's or a statement's of
  the caller's, with an assertion violation before anything reaches C. Allow a plain write, and
  document it as A does (recommended). The rule refuses a nested level too, where only the
  interrupt's hazard applies.
- **C. Refuse every convenience that can write inside a fold's procedure on the same
  connection:** `execute!`, `execute-script!` and `with-transaction`. `query` and `query-row` stay
  allowed.
- **D. Read the fold's rows before calling its procedure,** so no read is held while it runs. The
  fold then holds its rows in memory, as `query` does, and no longer streams.

**Recommendation: B.** `with-transaction` is the one verb whose promises can't hold beside a held
row: that its transaction never outlives it (Q6), and that its `BEGIN` waits under contention
(Q5's reason). Refusing keeps both, raises before anything reaches C, names the cause, and can be
loosened later. A plain write promises only its own raise, which it still gives. The pattern the
refusal points to, `with-transaction` around the fold, works (P12). B covers the statement tier
too, where P7 and P9 apply alike. Its cost: a `with-transaction` per row inside a fold is
refused, and such code reads with `query`, then loops. The check reads the connection's tracked
statements' states, one pass in the section that reads autocommit.
**Evidence:** slice-0 P7, P9, P12; concurrency I3; the plan's review (H2).

**Answer: "Refuse with-transaction"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). `with-transaction` refuses to
begin while its connection holds a row, a fold's or a statement's, with an assertion violation
before anything reaches C. Plain writes are allowed, and documented to fail at once under
contention. Declined: allow both and document; refuse every writer; a fold that reads all its
rows first.

### Q15. How much of a row, or of a statement's parameters, one critical section copies

Raised by M2's closing review, after Q14. A value crosses between Scheme and C byte by byte, inside
a critical section, with this thread's interrupts, timers and engines held off for the copy. M2's
closing review measured it on M2's verbs: a 64 MiB BLOB `column-value` took 1932 ms, and a 64 MiB
`bind!` 2000 ms, about 30 ns a byte. An engine reading one ran 1.8 s with no expiry, while another
thread's `collect-rendezvous` wasn't held (0 to 7 ms). So the question is how many copies one of
M3's sections holds:
- the row reader M2's session proposed (finding F8) reads every column in one section;
- `bind-all!`'s rule binds every parameter in one;
- either way a row with several big values holds this thread for their sum.

Read across several sections, a row stays consistent: nothing but its convenience can step a
checked-out statement, on the owner's thread (ADR-0010), and a column count changes only at a step
(single K2.2, K2.7). What a section costs through the library isn't measured; P8 measures it in
slice 3.

- **A. One section per row, and per statement's parameters,** as M2 proposed: the fewest sections,
  and this thread held off for the sum of a row's, or a statement's, copies.
- **B. One section per value, for reads and binds alike** (recommended): this thread is held off
  for one value's copy at most, as M2's own `column-value` and `bind!` already hold it, at the cost
  of a section per column and per parameter. `bind-all!`'s rule is kept by checking every value in
  Scheme before any is bound.
- **C. One section per row, with any value over a byte threshold copied in a section of its
  own:** few sections for small values, and a big value held alone. It needs a threshold, and the
  byte count read before the copy, in a section of its own.
- **D. As A, with the bound documented:** `docs/reference.md` says how long a row of big values
  holds this thread.

**Recommendation: B.** It holds this thread for the least time the copy allows, no worse than M2's
own verbs, and it is the simplest: each value through M2's who-taking column read and bind (F11),
with no row reader in M2's library. A row read across sections is consistent, by the reasons above.
Its cost, a section per value, is what P8 measures in slice 3; if it shows, C can replace B with an
amendment, and A stays the fallback.
**Evidence:** M2's closing review, its probes `big-read-section.sps` and `big-read-engine.sps`,
the review's own and not in the tree, whose cost ADR-0015's closing-review amendment records (PR #20); single K2.2, K2.7; ADR-0010,
decision 1.

**Answer: "One value each"** (the maintainer, 2026-11-15). One critical section per value, for
reads and binds, as M2's own verbs do; every value of a parameter list is checked in Scheme before
any is bound. Declined: one section per row; a row with big values copied alone; one per row,
documented.

## 4. Decisions that bind every slice

Each cites its evidence. Each is proposed in the record named.

### 4.1 The conveniences (ADR-0016)

- **Parameters are positional, after the SQL,** bound by `bind-all!`'s rule: every parameter, 1
  to n, from n values, each checked before any is bound (ADR-0015, decision 6). The brief's own
  example is `(query db "select … where id = ?" 42)` (brief §1). A named parameter binds by its
  index. Binding by name stays the statement tier's, `bind!`.
- **Each convenience finishes its statement on every path before it returns or raises:** back to
  the cache, reset with its bindings cleared, or finalized (ADR-0020). None is left at a row once
  it has returned or raised; `query-fold` holds its row only while its procedure runs. Because:
  - an interrupt sticks while any statement of the connection holds a row, and every new prepare
    and step answers 9 until it is reset (concurrency I3, I4b, trap 2; slice-0 P7);
  - `COMMIT` answers BUSY while the connection's own write is unfinished, such as an
    `insert … returning` that has answered a row (single O7, concurrency B11; M2's wording, PR
    #19);
  - with the connection's own read held, its `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and its writes answered BUSY at
    once, where without the held read they waited out a 1500 ms timeout (slice-0 P12);
  - `sqlite3_close` answers BUSY while a statement is unfinalized (single b5 V2).
- **A value is copied in a critical section of its own, for reads and binds alike**
  (Q15). A row is read one column at a time, through M2's who-taking column read
  (finding F11), and a statement's parameters bound one at a time, every value checked in Scheme
  before any is bound, as `bind-all!`'s rule has it. So this thread is held off for one value's
  copy at most, about 30 ns a byte (M2's closing review), never for a whole row's. A row read so
  stays consistent: nothing but its convenience can step a checked-out statement, on the owner's
  thread (ADR-0010), and its column count changes only at a step (single K2.2, K2.7), so the
  count is read once after the step. M2's session had proposed a row reader reading every column
  in one section (finding F8); Q15 asks which. Either way each read inherits slice 4's rule for a
  NULL pointer: a code 7 is NOMEM only when the column's type has turned NULL (ADR-0015's slice 4
  amendment).
- **Every raise names the convenience the caller called** (ADR-0013, decision 2), through
  who-taking internals in M2's two statement libraries, which M3 slice 3 adds as agreed with M2's
  session (finding F11).
- **A statement going back to the cache is reset, and the reset's own failure is raised:** the
  statement is finalized, not kept, and the failure raised after, unless the convenience is
  already raising a condition of its own, which then reaches the caller unchanged. `query-row` on
  an `insert … returning` of several rows, in autocommit, commits at that reset, which can answer
  BUSY and roll the insert back (ADR-0014's context), so the caller learns of it.
- **`execute!` steps to done and drops any rows.** `sqlite3_exec` does the same (single script
  X7), and a PRAGMA's set answers a row (single P1).
- **A row's columns are read at the row, after its step,** never counted at prepare: a column
  count changes at the step that re-prepares (single K2.1, K2.2, K2.7).
- **A row is a vector of its values, in column order** (Q2). **`query-row` answers `#f` for no
  row and the first row of several,** reset after it (Q3).
- **`execute!`, `query` and `query-row` call no procedure of the caller's,** and hold no
  `dynamic-wind`. A raise inside them is caught, the statement finished, and the raise made again
  after, the same object. So each is expected to complete under an engine at every fuel, as M2's
  prepare and step do (ADR-0014's slice 3 amendment). Slice 3 measures it (probe P6).
  - **Not every way out is a raise.** A timer's or a keyboard interrupt's handler that escapes,
    or an engine that expires inside one and is never resumed, leaves the checked-out statement
    at its row: out of the cache, still tracked and counted until the close finalizes it. While
    it holds the row, an interrupt sticks on its connection, a write's `COMMIT` answers BUSY, and
    every `with-transaction` on the connection is refused (§4.2): the caller can't reset a
    statement it never held, since `tracked-statements` is private, so the remedy is to close the
    connection. `docs/reference.md` says so. A `dynamic-wind` would finish the statement at every
    engine expiry instead, and the resume would step a statement reset under it.
- **`query-fold` calls the caller's procedure with its statement at a row.** Its shape follows
  M2's `call-with-statement` (`statement.sls:444–477`) where M2's holds, and adds a flag of M3's
  own where it doesn't:
  - **as M2's:** a plain `dynamic-wind` around a critical one (`dynamic-wind #t`). The plain
    winder's before thunk does nothing, so a re-entry goes through: a guard whose clauses don't
    match re-enters the raise's extent to raise again, and a before thunk that refused would put
    its own condition in place of the caller's (`connection.sls:465–470`, `statement.sls:436`; the
    plan's review measured it on Chez 10.4.0). The critical winder's after thunk finishes the
    statement, with interrupts disabled, under a guard that records any failure and raises
    nothing. The plain winder's after thunk raises that failure, unless a raise is under way;
  - **the hand-off sits inside a section:** the statement, a cache hit or a fresh prepare, is put
    in the fold's variable inside the section that checks it out or prepares it, as
    `call-with-statement`'s prepare learns its statement inside its own section (`:437–438`). So no
    escaping interrupt falls between the check-out and the after thunk's knowing of it;
  - **the raise record covers the wind's whole body,** the fold's own check-out, binds, steps and
    row reads as well as the procedure, through a handler that records a raise and hands it on in
    place, as M2's handler around its procedure does. So a failure of the fold's own counts as a
    raise, and a finish's failure is never raised over it;
  - **M3's own: the finish acts once, behind a flag.** M2's finish is idempotent through its
    resource: a finalized statement or a closed connection makes a second do nothing. The fold's
    isn't: a statement given back to the cache may already be another caller's. So the critical
    after thunk acts only on the first exit, and sets the flag;
  - **on a normal return the statement is at done, and goes back to the cache; on any other exit,
    a raise or an escape, it is finalized, never kept;**
  - **the loop reads the flag before every step and every row read, and after every call of the
    procedure.** Once it is set, the loop raises `&sqlite-closed` from `query-fold` without
    touching the statement, which by then may be in the cache, or another caller's. So a fold
    re-entered after any exit, a continuation captured in the procedure and called after the fold
    returned, or a procedure resumed by an outer handler that returned to a `raise-continuable`
    past a guard that re-entered, is refused at once, and never restarts from the first row. The
    plan's second review measured why: a fold shaped without this read, re-entered after a normal
    return, stepped the statement the cache held from its first row again, answered 4 rows
    after 6, and left it in the cache at done, so the next check-out's `step!` was refused. A
    slice 3 row holds it: the fold, re-entered after its normal return, raises `&sqlite-closed`,
    and the cache's statement is still `ready` and unstepped, read through `tracked-statements` and
    `statement-state`;
  - **an escape that isn't a raise** raises the finish's failure from the plain after thunk, as
    `call-with-statement` raises its finalize's. Under an engine's expiry that loses the engine, as
    ADR-0012's amendment measured for `call-with-database`'s close. An expiry also finishes the
    statement, finalizing it, so the resumed loop reads the flag and raises `&sqlite-closed`:
    `query-fold` doesn't survive an engine's preemption, and `docs/reference.md` says to take the
    statement tier there;
  - **while the procedure runs, its connection holds the fold's read.** So a write in the
    procedure answers BUSY at once under another's write lock, where without the held read it
    waited out a 1500 ms timeout, in both journal modes (slice-0 P12), and in WAL a
    `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` there can answer 517 (slice-0 P9). An interrupt made while it runs sticks
    until the fold ends (concurrency I3), and a `with-transaction` there then couldn't prepare its
    rollback (slice-0 P7). `with-transaction` inside a fold's procedure is refused (Q14); a plain
    write is allowed, and `docs/reference.md` says to run the fold inside `with-transaction`, which
    takes the write lock first, when its procedure writes: there the writes and savepoints ran at
    once (slice-0 P12).
- **`execute-script!`** (Q4):
  - **a loop of prepares, never `sqlite3_exec`,** through a private prepare that answers the
    first statement and its tail's offset (finding F1). Each statement is prepared only after the
    one before it has run, since preparing a PRAGMA applies it (single P3). Both measured runners
    do this (single script X1);
  - **it drops rows** (single script X7) and **stops at the first error.** The statements before
    it stand, and no transaction is begun, ended or rolled back by the runner (single script X1 to
    X4, X16);
  - **SQL holding no statement is no error:** empty, only semicolons, only comments (single script
    X9 to X12). **A NULL statement ends the loop:** its tail was the input's end in every case,
    and leading semicolons and comments went into the next statement's text instead, as in
    `;; select 2`, `-- c\n; select 2` and a U+FEFF then `; select 2`, by `prepare_v3` and
    `prepare_v2` alike, on both libraries (slice-0 P11);
  - **its `&sqlite-error` carries the whole script as `sql`, and an `offset` into it, in
    characters, that says which statement failed** (ADR-0013, amended): for a prepare's error,
    the token SQLite points at, since the loop's prepare gives the offset from where it started,
    and `sqlite3_exec`'s counts from the failing statement (single script X5, T10); for a step's
    error, which SQLite gives no offset, the failing statement's first token, past the
    whitespace, comments and semicolons before it, by ADR-0014's scan. **Both are rebuilt, not
    raised as they came:** the loop takes the prepare's and the step's answers as data, the
    extended code and the message read straight after the call, through M2's who-taking internals
    (finding F11), and makes a new `&sqlite-error` from them with the script and the offset, by
    `raise-sqlite-error`, which recomputes its class from the primary code (ADR-0013, amended), so
    a BUSY is still `&sqlite-busy`;
  - a script holding U+0000 is refused before anything is allocated (ADR-0011, amended). It is
    encoded once, into one block held for the loop by `call-with-c-string`, whose before thunk
    refuses a re-entry (M1's `call-with-block`). **So nothing raises out of the block's extent:**
    every raise in the loop is caught inside it and answered as data, and raised after
    `call-with-c-string` returns: SQLite's failures rebuilt, as above, and anything else, an
    assertion violation of M2's checks among them, the same object. A non-matching guard's re-raise would
    otherwise be replaced by that refusal: the plan's review measured `call-with-c-string` so,
    where `call-with-statement` and `call-with-database` kept the caller's condition (finding
    F13). The one re-entry left is an engine's resume after an expiry freed the block, which
    `call-with-block` refuses, so `execute-script!` doesn't survive an engine's preemption. It
    uses no cache, and prepares with no flags.
- **The counters:** `changes` reads `sqlite3_changes64`, `total-changes` reads
  `sqlite3_total_changes64`, `last-insert-rowid` reads `sqlite3_last_insert_rowid`, and
  `in-transaction?` is `sqlite3_get_autocommit` answering 0. Each checks its database, its owner
  and that it is open, as every verb does, and makes its one plain call through
  `call-with-open-handle`. `docs/reference.md` gives what each counts, measured (single B2):
  - `changes` is the last INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE's: DDL, SELECT, PRAGMA, `BEGIN` and `COMMIT`
    leave it unchanged (C3, C4); a `RETURNING` statement moves it only at done or reset (C12,
    C12b, C12c);
  - `total-changes` counts trigger and cascade rows, and rows later rolled back (C9, C10, C15);
  - `last-insert-rowid` can name a row a failure or a `ROLLBACK` undid (C5, C15, T7), and doesn't
    move for a WITHOUT ROWID table or an upsert's DO UPDATE (C8, C11);
  - another connection's writes move none of them (C13);
  - `in-transaction?` is `#t` after a deferred `BEGIN` that has read nothing, and `#f` while an
    autocommit SELECT holds a read (single B1, items 1 and 2).

### 4.2 Transactions (ADR-0017)

```scheme
(with-transaction db (lambda () (execute! db "insert …" 1)))              ; BEGIN IMMEDIATE
(with-transaction db 'deferred (lambda () (query db "select …")))
```

- **Outer or nested is read from `sqlite3_get_autocommit`.** At 1, `with-transaction` issues
  `BEGIN` with its mode, `immediate` by default (Q5); at 0, a savepoint. So a
  transaction the caller began with `execute!` nests too, and its `RELEASE` commits nothing (single
  S5). A nested call's mode has no effect: `SAVEPOINT` takes none (single S1). Reading autocommit
  first means `with-transaction` never issues a `BEGIN` inside a transaction, so a refused
  `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` never takes its lock (single traps T1).
- **Its savepoints are its own,** named `chez_sqlite3_N` by their depth on the connection, an
  ASCII identifier needing no quoting. A caller never names one, so nothing about SQLite's
  savepoint names reaches the caller: their ASCII-only case folding, their quoting forms, and a
  reused name reaching the most recent (single S8, S9). **N follows `with-transaction`'s own
  extents:** it drops when the call exits, whatever SQLite kept, so a sibling at the same depth
  reuses the name above a savepoint a failed `RELEASE` left open, and acts only on its own
  (ADR-0017, amended in M3 slice 3, the maintainer's "Reuse N").
- **The statements it issues:** `BEGIN [DEFERRED | IMMEDIATE | EXCLUSIVE]`, `COMMIT` and
  `ROLLBACK` for the outer level; `SAVEPOINT chez_sqlite3_N`, `RELEASE chez_sqlite3_N`, and, to
  roll back, `ROLLBACK TO chez_sqlite3_N` then `RELEASE chez_sqlite3_N`, since `ROLLBACK TO` leaves
  its savepoint open (single S2 to S4). Each runs through `execute!`'s path and the cache.
- **It refuses to begin while any statement of its connection is `row` or `failed`** (Q14). The
  states are M2's, read through `tracked-statements` and `statement-state`, a pass in Scheme that
  reaches no C, made first in the section that then reads autocommit, inside
  `call-with-open-handle`'s, on the owner's thread. The refusal is an assertion violation raised
  after that section, before anything reaches C. The pass is race-free as it stands: no other
  thread can step the connection's statements, and nested levels are refused with the rest.
  - **Why `row`:** beside a held read its `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` can't wait under the busy timeout
    (slice-0 P12, P9), and once an interrupt sticks behind the row its rollback can't be prepared
    (slice-0 P7). So it would break Q6's promise that its transaction never outlives it.
  - **Why `failed`:** M2's `failed` covers statements SQLite still holds active. The plan's second
    review measured it on both libraries. After a column read's NOMEM, which slice 4's
    `read-outcome` turns into `failed`, and after a step that answered BUSY, `sqlite3_stmt_busy`
    read 1. After an interrupt a prepare answered 9 only while another statement held a row: an
    interrupted statement alone read `stmt_busy` 0, and the next prepare answered 0 (ADR-0017,
    amended in M3 slice 3). Beside the NOMEM one, a `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` answered 5 at once against
    a 600 ms timeout; beside the BUSY one it waited. The controls, a step failing on a constraint
    and a statement at done, read 0, the `BEGIN` waited, and the prepare answered 0. A failed
    statement wants `reset!` anyway, so the refusal names it.
  - **A statement left at a row makes every later `with-transaction` on its connection refuse.**
    A timer's or a keyboard handler's escape, or an abandoned engine, can leave a convenience's
    statement checked out at its row until the close (§4.1), and the caller can't reset it, since
    `tracked-statements` is private. Then the refusal holds until the connection closes, which is
    the remedy. `docs/reference.md` says so, beside the refusal.
- **When the body raises, or leaves through a continuation,** the first such exit, by the flags
  below, reads autocommit, after the conveniences have finished their statements (§4.1). If the
  transaction is still there it rolls its level back; if not, it issues nothing. Then the body's
  condition reaches the caller unchanged. A failure of the rollback isn't raised over it, as
  `call-with-database`'s close isn't (ADR-0012, decision 8). The read is the one test (single B1,
  "Which errors end the transaction" and "NOMEM, FULL and INTERRUPT"):
  - the same NOMEM or FULL undid only its statement, or ended the transaction, by one function
    call, one constraint, one row, or whether a table was read (single F1 to F8, N1 to N4, M1 to
    M5);
  - `OR ROLLBACK` and `RAISE(ROLLBACK)` end it (single E4, E5, E7, E8); an interrupted write ends
    it and its savepoints, and an interrupted SELECT doesn't (concurrency I5, trap 3; single
    txn-special I1 to I4);
  - a column read's NOMEM, in a statement that reads a table, ends it at the reset or finalize
    after it (single txn-special G2, G3), which the conveniences run before the raise leaves
    them. One reading no table, as `select ?1`, keeps it (G4, G5).

  ROLLBACK succeeds with a statement mid-row, which then goes on answering rows (single O2, O7b,
  T2), or 516 after a rolled-back schema change (single O4b). `docs/reference.md` says so.
- **When the body returns, the first time,** it reads autocommit first. If the transaction has
  ended, it raises `&sqlite-transaction-ended` and issues nothing (Q7). Otherwise it issues
  `COMMIT` or `RELEASE`, and answers the body's values. A `COMMIT` that fails and leaves the transaction open
  is rolled back, then its failure raised (Q6).
  - **A `RELEASE` that fails with its savepoint still open is a failed `COMMIT` too:** the level is
    rolled back, `ROLLBACK TO` then `RELEASE`, then the first `RELEASE`'s failure is raised. A
    sticky interrupt makes one: `RELEASE` answered 9 at prepare beside a held row (slice-0 P7).
    The rollback then fails the same way, and the savepoint and its transaction stay open, which
    `in-transaction?` tells. The connection's own `INSERT … RETURNING` held at a row makes one
    too: `RELEASE` answered 5, `ROLLBACK TO` 101, and the rollback's `RELEASE` 5 again.
  - **A failed commit's rollback is tried once:** `ended` is set in the section of its last step,
    whatever that step answered, so the after thunk issues nothing more (ADR-0017, amended in M3
    slice 3, the maintainer's "Once").
- **The exits, decided by flags, never by autocommit alone.** Its shape follows M2's
  `call-with-database` (`connection.sls:478–520`) where M2's holds, and adds flags of M3's own
  where it doesn't:
  - **as M2's:** a plain `dynamic-wind` around a critical one. The plain winder's before thunk does
    nothing, so a re-entry goes through: a guard whose clauses don't match re-enters the raise's
    extent to raise again, and a refusing before thunk would replace the caller's condition with
    its own (`connection.sls:465–470`; measured by the plan's review on Chez 10.4.0). The critical
    winder's after thunk does the rollback, with interrupts disabled, under a guard that records
    any failure and raises nothing; the plain winder's after thunk raises that failure, unless a
    raise is under way. A handler around the wind's whole body records a raise and hands it on in
    place, as M2's handler around its procedure does, so the begin's and the commit's own
    failures count as raises too;
  - **the begin sits inside the wind's body, after the refusal,** as M2 puts its open inside the
    winder (`connection.sls:470–473`), so an escaping interrupt after the `BEGIN` still reaches the
    after thunk. **Two flags of M3's own say where the level stands:** `begun`, set inside the
    section of the step that answered the `BEGIN` or `SAVEPOINT`, as `call-with-statement`'s
    prepare learns its statement inside its own section (`statement.sls:437–438`), so no interrupt
    falls between them; and `ended`, set inside the section of the step that commits, releases or
    completes the rollback;
  - **the commit runs in the wind's body, after the body returns:** with `ended` still unset, the
    autocommit read, then `COMMIT` or `RELEASE`, Q6's and Q7's rules, and the body's values
    answered. Its raises, a failed commit's or `&sqlite-transaction-ended`, are made there, in the
    body, outside any critical section. **Each step that ends the level, the `COMMIT` or `RELEASE`
    and each step of a failed commit's rollback, reads `ended` inside its own section before it
    steps,** and with it set steps nothing: an engine's expiry between the autocommit read and the
    step runs the after thunk's rollback, and the resumed body would otherwise step what it had
    planned (ADR-0017, amended in M3 slice 1);
  - **the critical after thunk acts only when `begun` is set and `ended` isn't:** it reads
    autocommit, rolls its level back when the transaction is still there, and sets `ended`. M2's
    close is idempotent through its resource, a closed connection; a rollback isn't. A second
    `ROLLBACK TO chez_sqlite3_N` on a level already ended would answer "no such savepoint", or,
    with the outer transaction still open, roll back a newer savepoint at the same depth. So the
    flags decide;
  - **a later exit issues nothing.** If the body returns after its level has ended, it raises
    `&sqlite-transaction-ended` from the wind's body, Q7's rule applied to its own earlier exit,
    never a second `COMMIT` or `RELEASE`. If the later exit is a raise or an escape, nothing more
    is done. The cases:
    - **a body resumed** by an outer handler that returned to a `raise-continuable` past a guard
      that re-entered: its first exit rolled back, its later statements autocommit, as single L1
      measured, and its return raises `&sqlite-transaction-ended`;
    - **a continuation captured in the body and called after the commit:** the body returns again,
      and raises `&sqlite-transaction-ended`;
    - **an engine that expires inside the extent, then is resumed:** each expiry is an escape. One
      before `begun` is set does nothing, and the resume begins and commits as usual. One after it
      rolls back, and the resumed body's return raises `&sqlite-transaction-ended`. So at every
      fuel the call either commits once, or raises, and never commits silently what was rolled
      back. A slice 4 row sweeps fuel from 1 until the call completes without expiring: M3 slice
      1's review modelled the shape and found its window at fuels 138 to 174 (ADR-0017, amended).

    The plan's second review modelled the shape without these rules. It found every such return
    answering the body's values silently: `(body-returned-after-resume 42)`, `(body-returned time
    2)`, and a return after 400 expiries;
  - **on an escape that isn't a raise,** there is no condition to keep, so the rollback's failure
    is raised from the plain after thunk, as `call-with-statement` raises its finalize's. Under an
    engine's expiry that loses the engine, as ADR-0012's amendment measured for
    `call-with-database`'s close. So `with-transaction` doesn't survive an engine's preemption,
    and `docs/reference.md` says to issue `BEGIN` and `COMMIT` through `execute!` there.
- **A sticky interrupt makes the rollback fail.** While a statement of the caller's, from the
  statement tier, held a row after an interrupt, the prepares of `ROLLBACK`, `COMMIT`,
  `ROLLBACK TO` and `RELEASE` each answered 9 with no statement, and a `ROLLBACK` prepared before
  the interrupt, as the cache holds one, answered 9 at its step and again at its reset. The
  transaction stayed open, and another connection saw nothing committed. Once the held statement
  was reset, each ran normally. Alike on both libraries (slice-0 P7). So the body's condition is
  raised, the transaction stays open, and `in-transaction?` tells; the cached `ROLLBACK` that
  failed is finalized (§4.6). A `COMMIT` failing so is rolled back by Q6's rule, whose rollback
  fails the same way, and the commit's failure is raised with the transaction open. Both a
  statement of the caller's and `query-fold`'s own, while its procedure runs, hold such a row;
  the refusal above keeps `with-transaction` from beginning beside either, so only
  a row taken inside the body, by the statement tier, still reaches this. `docs/reference.md` says
  to reset such a statement first.
- **A read the connection holds defeats the busy timeout for its writes.** Alike on both
  libraries:
  - with the connection's own SELECT held at a row, under another's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and write,
    its `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and an autocommit INSERT answered BUSY at once, where without the held
    read they waited out a 1500 ms timeout, in WAL and rollback-journal mode alike (slice-0 P12);
  - in WAL, with the held SELECT and another connection's commit after it, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and
    `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE` answered 517, `busy-snapshot`, at once with a 300 ms timeout, and began no
    transaction; a deferred `BEGIN` succeeded, and its first write answered 517 (slice-0 P9);
  - with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` taken first and the SELECT held after it, an INSERT and a savepoint's
    `SAVEPOINT`, INSERT and `RELEASE` ran at once (slice-0 P12).

  So a `BEGIN` beside a held read would raise `&sqlite-busy` before the body runs, and Q5's
  default wouldn't help: the refusal above stops it first. `docs/reference.md`
  says to run a fold that writes inside `with-transaction`, which takes the lock before the
  fold's read.
- **DDL rolls back** with the rest (single D1, D2).

### 4.3 The settings list (ADR-0018)

```scheme
(open-database "app.db" '((journal-mode . wal) (foreign-keys . #t) (busy-timeout . 5000)))
(call-with-database "app.db" 'read-write '((busy-timeout . 250) (statement-cache . 0))
  (lambda (db) …))
```

- **The list is one argument among the options,** an association list from a setting's name to
  its value. ADR-0012 left it so: "M3's settings list arrives as an argument that isn't a
  symbol, so the options stay symbols." Two lists, a name given twice, an unknown name and a value
  outside its setting's domain are each an assertion violation, before anything is opened: SQLite
  would ignore an unknown or misspelt name, and coerce a bad value silently (single P4, P5, T4;
  concurrency B1).
- **The allowlist, and each value's domain:** SQLite's six (Q8), and one of the library's own,
  `statement-cache` (Q11):

  | Setting | Scheme value | Written as | Read back as |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `busy-timeout` | exact integer, 0 to 2³¹−1 ms | the integer | INTEGER, column `timeout` |
  | `foreign-keys` | `#t` or `#f` | `1` or `0` | INTEGER |
  | `journal-mode` | `delete`, `truncate`, `persist` or `wal` | the word | lower-case TEXT |
  | `synchronous` | `off`, `normal`, `full` or `extra` | `0` to `3` | INTEGER |
  | `temp-store` | `default`, `file` or `memory` | `0` to `2` | INTEGER |
  | `trusted-schema` | `#t` or `#f` | `1` or `0` | INTEGER |
  | `statement-cache` | exact integer, 0 to 1024 statements; 32 when absent | nothing: kept in the record | nothing: SQLite never sees it |

  Measured: `busy_timeout` keeps 2147483647 and makes 2147483648 and −5 into 0 (concurrency B1);
  `synchronous = 4` is stored and read back as 4, no level of SQLite's (single P4); `temp_store`
  makes 7, 3 and −1 into 0 (single P4); each reads back as the table says (single P1, P8, P9).
  `#t` and `#f` stand for on and off here: a setting is no stored value, so ADR-0015's reasons for
  refusing them don't arise. `journal-mode`'s `memory` and `off` are left out of v1: no research
  measured them on a file, and a value refused can be loosened later.
- **Left out, with the reason measured:** `page_size` and `auto_vacuum`, which wait for a VACUUM
  once the file holds a table (single P1, P12); `encoding`, which does nothing then (single P1,
  P12.3); `user_version` and `application_id`, which are the file's data (single P1); the heap
  limits, which are process-wide (single P2, T9); `defer_foreign_keys`, which switches itself off
  (single P6b); `case_sensitive_like`, which has no read form (single P1).
- **Each of SQLite's six is set, then read back by a second statement,** `PRAGMA name`, and
  compared with the value written. A set can answer no row, as `foreign_keys = 1` does
  (concurrency S3; single P1), so the set's own row is never the check. A mismatch raises
  `&sqlite-setting-mismatch`, with the setting, the value asked for and the value read, each as a
  Scheme value.
- **`statement-cache` is the library's own** (Q11). The read-back exists because SQLite coerces a
  value silently (single P4); SQLite never sees this one. It is checked in Scheme with the rest,
  before anything opens, and becomes the record's cache capacity when the record is made, before
  any PRAGMA runs. Nothing reads it back, and it can't mismatch. It isn't an open option, since
  options stay symbols (ADR-0012, decision 3), and a size is a number.
- **`busy-timeout` runs first,** then the rest in the list's order. Under another connection's
  lock, on a fresh connection with a 200 ms timeout set first, alike on both libraries (slice-0
  P2):
  - in rollback-journal mode, under an exclusive lock, `PRAGMA journal_mode`, `journal_mode =
    wal`, both forms of `synchronous`, and a SELECT waited out the timeout at prepare, then
    answered BUSY: the prepares that, with no lock, took 0.02 to 0.16 ms, against 0.006 ms at most
    for the rest. `foreign_keys`, `temp_store`, `trusted_schema` and `busy_timeout` ran at once,
    both forms;
  - under a read, `journal_mode = wal` waited out the timeout at its step; everything else ran;
  - **two refusals came at once, with the 200 ms timeout:** `journal_mode = wal` under another's
    reserved lock, and `journal_mode = delete` on a WAL file beside another connection that has
    read it, even one idle now; one opened and never used doesn't block it (M3 slice 5's
    research, `locks.sps`);
  - in WAL mode nothing else waited;
  - **on a file already in WAL, `journal_mode = wal` ran at once and read back `wal`,** beside
    another's read, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` or `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE` (slice-0 P14). So the connections of a
    WAL file can all carry `(journal-mode . wal)`, as the contention suite's do, and each opens
    while the others hold locks.

  When the other connection let go mid-wait, the waiting call succeeded: `PRAGMA journal_mode`
  under an exclusive lock, and `journal_mode = wal` under a read. It returned 28.7 to 29.1 ms
  after the release, medians, and 34.4 to 34.7 ms for `journal_mode = wal`, on both libraries
  (slice-0 P3). That fits the busy handler's schedule of sleeps, read from the floor's source
  (concurrency B3), whose retries fall at 128 and 178 ms; the schedule is inferred, not measured
  on its own. So the order buys a setting that waits for a short lock instead of failing at
  once.
- **The list runs at every open, and only there** (brief §2.7). At open the connection has no
  statement and no transaction, so neither a reader of its own (single P13) nor a transaction
  (single P6, T5; concurrency B8) can make a setting fail or do nothing. After open, a PRAGMA is SQL,
  through `execute!`.
- **A setting that fails, or mismatches, closes the connection, then raises,** as a failed open
  closes its handle before it raises (ADR-0012, decision 4). `call-with-database` never calls its
  procedure then. A close that fails then isn't raised over the setting's condition: the
  maintainer's "Setting's error wins", 2026-11-16 (ADR-0018, amended in M3 slice 5).
- **An open with settings runs statements,** each in sections of its own, so an engine can
  preempt between them. `open-database` holds no `dynamic-wind` around them, so an expiry
  suspends the open and its resume goes on. ADR-0012's amendment measured the open alone
  completing at every fuel from 1 to 60. Slice 5 swept the open with settings until it
  completed unexpired, at fuel 221, and one remainder's runs interleaved. Each call holds a
  claim that every section reads and advances, so a run the call has passed touches nothing
  (ADR-0018, amended in M3 slices 1 and 5).
- **`journal-mode` on an in-memory database mismatches** (Q9): it answers `memory`
  (single P11; concurrency S4).
- **The read-back runs on the connection that set the value:** another connection's
  `journal_mode` can be stale until its next read (concurrency B8, trap 5).
- **The settings' statements never use the cache:** each runs once, so the cache would hold it for
  nothing.
- **No default busy timeout** (Q10).

### 4.4 Interrupt and close (ADR-0019)

```scheme
;; on a watchdog thread
(interrupt-database! db)
```

- **Any thread may call `interrupt-database!`.** It checks that its argument is a database, an
  assertion violation otherwise, and makes no owner check: it is ADR-0010's one exception.
- **The record gains a mutex and an `interruptible` flag.** The interrupt, inside one critical
  section, takes the mutex, calls `sqlite3_interrupt` if the flag is set, and releases it. The
  close, inside its section and before it finalizes anything, takes the mutex, clears the flag and
  releases it, then finalizes and closes. **A close that leaves the connection open sets the flag
  again,** on both such paths: `sqlite3_close` failing (`connection.sls:428`), and a statement
  found untracked, refused before `sqlite3_close` runs (`connection.sls:417`). The open sets it
  once the record exists.
  - **The interrupt calls `sqlite3_interrupt` while it holds the mutex,** and the close clears the
    flag while it holds the same mutex. So an interrupt already in flight when the close begins
    finishes before the flag is cleared, and none starts after: no interrupt is in flight once
    `sqlite3_close` starts. SQLite calls `sqlite3_interrupt` unsafe on a connection that is closed
    or might close before it returns (both headers; concurrency A4).
  - **The mutex is the exclusion, never the section.** A critical section keeps out only its own
    thread's engines, timers and interrupts: M2's closing review measured another thread run
    423,841,665 loop iterations while one thread sat inside a section, and neither
    `connection.sls` nor `statement.sls` holds a mutex. The section around the mutex keeps this
    thread from being preempted while it holds the mutex, so no other thread waits on an engine's
    resume.
  - **Against M2's closing review's suggestion:** a mutex taken inside the close's section around
    `sqlite3_close` and the clearing of the handle, which the interrupt takes too. Both keep an
    interrupt off a freed connection. M3 keeps its own shape, clearing the flag before the close's
    finalizes and closing with the mutex released, because `sqlite3_close` can write a checkpoint,
    about 5 ms for 4 MB of WAL (concurrency S5), more for a larger one, and an interrupting thread,
    a watchdog among them, would wait on that I/O. Its cost: an interrupt made during the close's
    finalizes is dropped, where M2's shape would deliver it. None of the close's work is a step or a
    prepare, the calls an interrupt was measured to stop (concurrency I1, I7); what one would do to
    a finalize isn't measured.
  - **The mutex is held only inside a critical section, for constant time, never across a call
    that can block,** as the counters' mutex is (ADR-0005, decision 4). A thread in a section
    doesn't stop for another's collection until it leaves (ADR-0012's closing-review amendment
    measured a collection waiting behind one), so a holder always releases the mutex before it
    stops, and neither side waits on a collection the other blocks. `sqlite3_close` can take
    milliseconds, writing a checkpoint (concurrency S5), and runs with the mutex released.
- **On a closed connection, or one closing, it does nothing,** and raises nothing. A watchdog's
  interrupt races its target's close by nature; a raise there would depend on timing.
- **`sqlite3_interrupt` is called alone, bound plain.** It took 0.31 to 12.22 µs, medians 0.45 to
  0.60, and didn't wait for the connection's `FULLMUTEX`, where another thread's `sqlite3_errmsg`
  waited 200 ms for a running step (concurrency I1, I8). So the verb reads nothing else of the
  connection.
- **What it reaches,** which `docs/reference.md` gives:
  - a running step stops within 0.14 ms, and a long prepare within about 6 ms, each answering 9
    (concurrency I1, I7, C1);
  - **not a wait in the busy handler,** which ends at its timeout (concurrency I6, C2, S6, trap 1);
  - a statement held at a row counts as running, and the interrupt sticks until it is reset
    (concurrency I3, I4b, trap 2);
  - one that lands between statements is lost, and a watchdog repeats it until the work stops
    (Q12; concurrency I2);
  - an interrupted write ends the transaction and its savepoints, and an interrupted SELECT
    doesn't (concurrency I5, trap 3).
- **An interrupt raises `&sqlite-interrupt`,** a new subtype of `&sqlite-error` classed by its
  primary code `interrupt`, since a caller who interrupts will catch it (ADR-0013, decision 8,
  amended).
- **`sqlite3_is_interrupted` is neither bound nor exported:** it reads 1 after an interrupt that
  reached nothing, until the next statement starts (concurrency I2).
- **No deadline and no watchdog in v1.** An engine's fuel and a timer can't bound time spent
  inside SQLite: their ticks don't elapse during a foreign call (concurrency C3). A caller who
  wants a deadline runs a thread that calls `interrupt-database!`.
- **Every other section in M3 needs no lock between threads, and none is read as one.** Each
  guards state only its connection's owner thread touches, by ADR-0010's decision 1, so the only
  rivals are this thread's own engines and interrupts, which a section does keep out:
  - the statement cache's table and its capacity (§4.6);
  - M2's table of tracked statements, which the cache's statements live in;
  - `with-transaction`'s refusal pass, its `begun` and `ended` flags, and the savepoints' depth
    (§4.2);
  - `query-fold`'s flag, and the statement it holds (§4.1);
  - the settings' application at open (§4.3).

  `interrupt-database!` is the one call another thread makes, and it touches only the
  `interruptible` flag and the handle, under the record's mutex.
- **The exclusion is held by a test whatever the gate can see.** Calling `sqlite3_interrupt` on a
  closed connection is undefined behaviour, so its mutant may pass under Valgrind (probe P5).
  So the exclusion is a procedure of its own, which takes the interrupt's foreign call as an
  argument, as M2 slice 1's NULL-pointer readers take theirs. Slice 1 tests it with stand-ins: a
  stand-in call that records when it ran, against a close's clearing of the flag on another
  thread, every wait bounded. A mutant without the mutex, or one that clears the flag after
  `sqlite3_close`, lets the stand-in run once the close has begun, and fails the row. The gate's
  race row in slice 6 is then a second check, not the only one.

### 4.5 The busy timeout (ADR-0019)

- **It is a setting, `busy-timeout`,** set by PRAGMA and read back (§4.3). `sqlite3_busy_timeout`
  and `PRAGMA busy_timeout` are one setting, and only the PRAGMA can be read (concurrency B1), so
  the C call isn't bound.
- **No busy handler and no retry in Scheme** (brief §4). A busy wait ends at its timeout,
  interrupt or not (concurrency I6).
- **No new busy subtypes.** ADR-0013 classes `busy` and `locked` together as `&sqlite-busy`, and
  the extended code already tells the two cases that differ: `busy-snapshot` (517) and `locked`
  (6) (concurrency B5, B9). The four BUSY cases that need different remedies all answer 5,
  extended 5 (concurrency B2, B3, B7, B11), so no subtype could tell them apart.
  `docs/reference.md` gives each remedy.
- **A test times a wait with a timeout of at least 1500 ms, asserting at least 1000 ms, and under
  twice the timeout.** A build without `nanosleep` sleeps whole seconds: under 1000 ms it answers
  BUSY at once, and 1500 waits 1000 (concurrency B3's variant). No compile option shows it. The
  upper bound fails a retry in Scheme; under Valgrind a 1500 ms wait ran 1509 to 1526 ms
  (ADR-0019, amended in M3 slice 5; AGENTS.md).

### 4.6 The statement cache (ADR-0020)

- **One per connection, keyed by the SQL's exact text,** a copy the caller can't reach: a
  statement is filed under its own SQL copy, the one M2's record holds and never changes, and a
  lookup uses a copy of the caller's string, made before the section, so a caller changing its
  string can't corrupt the table (ADR-0011, decision 2). It holds at most N statements, the least
  recently used out first, N the connection's `statement-cache` setting: 32 when the settings
  give none, up to 1024 (Q11). An evicted statement is finalized. N is fixed when
  the record is made, at open, so the cache never shrinks under statements it holds.
- **At 0 the cache is off.** Every convenience, and `with-transaction`'s own statements, prepares
  its statement with no flags, as `prepare-statement` does, and finalizes it when done, on every
  path, as §4.1 has it finish one. Nothing else changes: a convenience behaves the same, and only
  its cost differs (probe P8).
- **Used by `execute!`, `query`, `query-row`, `query-fold` and `with-transaction`'s own
  statements;** never by `prepare-statement`, `call-with-statement`, `execute-script!` or the
  settings.
- **While N > 0, its statements are prepared with `SQLITE_PREPARE_PERSISTENT`.** Without it, 50 held statements
  filled the connection's 123 lookaside slots, and the misses grew; with it, a held statement took
  no lookaside, and 1.8 to 2.3 KB of heap instead (single K1). It made a prepare 0.36 to 0.81 µs
  dearer, and a reuse no cheaper or dearer, within 50 ns (single cost). So it costs only at a miss.
- **A statement is checked out while in use,** and absent from the cache meanwhile: a fold's
  procedure that runs the same SQL gets a fresh one. **Returned,** it is reset and its bindings
  cleared, and it takes its place as the most recent; **a return into a full cache evicts the least
  recently used statement,** and the returned one stays. It is finalized instead only when:
  - N is 0;
  - it is `query-fold`'s, leaving by any exit but a normal return (§4.1);
  - the cache already holds that SQL, returned meanwhile;
  - its step or a read left it `failed`;
  - its reset or its clearing raised. The failure is then raised after the convenience's own work,
    unless the convenience is already raising its own condition (§4.1).

  The cache trusts ADR-0014's states, never `sqlite3_stmt_busy`, which reads 1 after an `EXPLAIN`
  answers done (single K3, T11).
- **A statement whose step failed is never kept:** after another connection rebuilt its table, a
  held INSERT went on answering "table t has 3 columns" until another statement read the schema
  (single K2.10, T6). Finalized, the next call prepares afresh. A statement that re-prepares
  without failing keeps its place: after `CREATE INDEX` it kept its bindings (single K2.1, K2.6),
  and after a column was added its count changed at that step (K2.2, K2.7).
- **Every call of a convenience holds a flag,** set once its statement leaves the call, and read
  first in every section that touches the statement, the check-out's and the return's among
  them: Chez's engine remainders are multi-shot, and a timer's handler can capture a
  continuation between any two sections, so a second resume would otherwise hand a statement
  out twice (ADR-0016 and ADR-0020, amended in M3 slice 3, the maintainer's "Guard every
  call").
- **Every change to the cache is made inside a critical section that raises nothing and answers
  data,** so two engines sharing a connection can't interleave a check-out with a return:
  - **before the section,** the database is checked to be one, on its owner's thread, and the SQL
    to be a string, and copied, so the table's lookup can't raise and the key can't change;
  - **in the section,** inside `call-with-open-handle`'s, the connection is checked open first, and
    the table is only read and changed: a check-out answers a statement or a miss, a return
    answers the statement it evicted, if any;
  - **after it,** an evicted statement is finalized by M2's finalize. It is reset, so its finalize
    has nothing to commit; a failure of its own is raised all the same, after the convenience's own
    work, under the rule for a reset's failure above;
  - a slice 3 row sweeps two engines sharing a connection over fuel from 1 until both complete
    without expiring (ADR-0020, amended in M3 slice 1), each running the same
    conveniences on the same SQL, as M1's closing review swept `call-with-open-handle`: no
    statement is handed out twice, finalized twice or lost, each answer is right, and the
    `statement` count ends where it began. A mutant without the section fails it.
- **A checked-out or cached statement that is finalized is a miss.** A finalized record is never
  handed out, so no convenience can be given a statement the close already freed.
- **The close empties the cache, whether or not the connection closes.** `close-database!`
  finalizes every tracked statement, the cache's among them, before `sqlite3_close`, which would
  otherwise answer BUSY (single b5 V2; ADR-0014, decision 8). They are reset, with no commit
  pending, so their finalize answers `SQLITE_OK`. The same section then clears the record's cache.
  A close that leaves the connection open (`connection.sls:417`, a statement untracked;
  `connection.sls:428`, `sqlite3_close` failing) has finalized them all the same, so a cache kept
  through it would hold freed statements.
- **A PRAGMA statement is cached like any other.** Reset and stepped again, with no prepare, each
  of the six settings, `cache_size` and `user_version` applied its setting again, with flags 0 and
  with `PERSISTENT`, alike on both libraries: SQLite re-prepared it at that step, its re-prepare
  count going from 0 to 1 (slice-0 P1). It did so with nothing run between its two steps (P1b),
  and after the value was changed by the C call `sqlite3_busy_timeout`, which no PRAGMA statement
  made (P1c). The control, a SELECT stepped twice with nothing between, didn't re-prepare
  (slice-0 P1d). So a cached PRAGMA re-applies, and the cache needs no rule of its own for it.
  Caching one saved no prepare at its second step, since SQLite prepared it again there.
- **Any set of `foreign_keys` or `trusted_schema` expires every other statement on the
  connection.** Run between a held SELECT's two steps, each of `foreign_keys = 1`, `= 0` and
  `= off`, and `trusted_schema = 0` and `= 1`, made the SELECT re-prepare at its next step,
  with the value changed and with it already so. The read forms of the six, the set forms of the
  other four, `cache_size`, `SELECT 1` and nothing at all didn't. Alike on both libraries
  (slice-0 P13, P13b; the plan's second review found the other values first). So
  `(execute! db "PRAGMA foreign_keys = 1")` makes every statement the cache holds re-prepare once
  at its next use. That is right, not stale, and only costs a prepare each, which slice 3's cost
  probe (P8) keeps out of its loops: no set of either runs there. `docs/reference.md` names it.
- **What the cache saves through the library is measured in slice 3** (probe P8), against the
  same calls at a capacity of 0. Through bare FFI, reuse saved 1.9 to 2.3 µs a statement, a lower
  bound (single cost).
- **Slice 3 sets the capacity privately.** The record's capacity is built in slice 3, with a
  private way for the suites to make a connection of a given capacity, so slice 3's rows at 0, its
  cost rows and §4.8's 50-statement plant need no settings list. Slice 5 adds the public
  `statement-cache` setting, which reaches the same field, and its own rows.

### 4.8 What M3 plants

Each becomes a test, watched failing against a planted copy:
- a convenience that leaves its statement at a row (a later prepare answers 9 after an interrupt);
- `with-transaction` rolling back without reading autocommit, in single E4's case. Its body's
  condition is raised either way, since a failed rollback is never raised over it, so the row
  reads what differs: the connection's extended code after `with-transaction` raised, through
  native's `sqlite3_extended_errcode` and `call-with-open-handle` as suites reach them, is the
  insert's 2067 with the read, and the refused `ROLLBACK`'s 1 without it; and the cached
  `ROLLBACK` from an earlier use is still among the connection's tracked statements, read by
  `tracked-statements` and `statement-sql`, with the read, and finalized without it;
- `with-transaction` committing after its transaction ended (single L1);
- a before thunk that refuses a re-entry, in `query-fold` and `with-transaction`: under a guard
  whose clauses don't match, inside one that catches everything, the outer guard must catch the
  body's own condition, not `re-entered` (H1);
- a raise let out of `execute-script!`'s block, instead of caught inside its extent: under the
  same guards, the outer guard would catch `call-with-block`'s refusal, not the script's
  `&sqlite-error`. (`call-with-block` refuses a re-entry already, so planting a refusing before
  thunk there would change nothing);
- `query-fold`'s loop without its read of the flag, or a finish that acts twice: the fold,
  re-entered through a continuation after its normal return, must raise `&sqlite-closed`, and the
  statement the cache holds must still be `ready` and unstepped, read through `tracked-statements`
  and `statement-state`. The mutants step it from its first row and leave it at done (the plan's
  second review measured that shape);
- `with-transaction` acting twice, where the double act shows. A second finalize does nothing,
  and a second outer rollback reads autocommit 1 and issues nothing, so those rows can't fail;
  these can:
  - a nested level re-entered by a non-matching guard: the mutant's second
    `ROLLBACK TO chez_sqlite3_N` fails, "no such savepoint", so its cached statement is finalized
    and the connection's extended code becomes 1. The row reads the extended code, the body's own,
    and the cached `ROLLBACK TO` among the tracked statements;
  - a guard whose clause's test begins a transaction, then answers `#f`, between the two exits: the
    mutant's second rollback ends that transaction, and the row expects it still open, by
    `in-transaction?`;
- `with-transaction` deciding a later exit by autocommit, not by its flags: a resumed body, a
  continuation called after the commit, and an engine resumed after an expiry must each raise
  `&sqlite-transaction-ended`; the mutant returns the body's values, or issues `RELEASE` on a
  released savepoint;
- the cache ignoring its `statement-cache` setting, or evicting one too early or too late (the
  `statement` count after 33 distinct statements, at 0, 1, 2 and the default);
- the cache keeping a statement whose step failed (single traps T6's case), and preparing without
  `PERSISTENT` (a suite that binds `sqlite3_db_status` itself, as M2's binds `sqlite3_limit`, reads
  `LOOKASIDE_USED` full for 50 held statements: single K1);
- a setting applied without its read-back (`:memory:`'s `wal`), and a value passed to SQL
  unchecked (`synchronous = 4`);
- `interrupt-database!` without the close's exclusion, or clearing the flag after
  `sqlite3_close`: caught by slice 1's stand-in row (§4.4). SQLite calls `sqlite3_interrupt` unsafe
  on a closed connection (both headers); whether the gate also reports the planted copy is probe
  P5. **As built in slice 6:** the latch's own rows can't see the record's wiring, so the slice's
  review found a call made after the latch returns passing every row. `test-interrupt-database`
  now sweeps `interrupt-database/with` in an engine on a stand-in, and `interrupt-database!`
  under the gate, and holds a stand-in in flight across two threads (ADR-0019, amended in M3
  slice 6).

### 4.9 The surface

```scheme

;; execute-script! : Database String -> Void

;;
;; --- the settings (ADR-0018), amending ADR-0012's two ---
;; open-database : String (U Symbol Settings) ... -> Database
;; call-with-database : String (U Symbol Settings) ... (Database -> A ...) -> A ...
;; Settings = (Listof (Pairof Symbol Any))
;;
;; --- interrupt (ADR-0019) ---

;;
;; --- conditions (ADR-0013, amended) ---
;; sqlite-interrupt? : Any -> Boolean
;; sqlite-setting-mismatch? : Any -> Boolean
;; sqlite-setting-mismatch-setting : SqliteSettingMismatch -> Symbol
;; sqlite-setting-mismatch-expected : SqliteSettingMismatch -> Any
;; sqlite-setting-mismatch-actual : SqliteSettingMismatch -> Any
;; sqlite-transaction-ended? : Any -> Boolean
;;
;; Value = (U Integer Flonum String Bytevector SqlNull), as ADR-0015 has it
```

The names are the maintainer's (Q1). `test-public` holds the list whole, and `test-reference` holds
`docs/reference.md`'s signatures to these comments. **Each signature is one line, `;; NAME :
TYPE`, with nothing after it:** `test-reference` takes a line's whole text after `NAME : ` as the
type, and matches it, character for character, against the one line of the entry's text block
(`tests/test-reference.sps:220–228`, `262–285`). So what the comments used to say beside the
signatures is said here:
- `query-fold` calls `(proc acc row)`, as R6RS `fold-left` does;
- `with-transaction`'s mode is `'immediate` when absent;
- at most one of the options is a `Settings` list, whose names are `busy-timeout`,
  `foreign-keys`, `journal-mode`, `synchronous`, `temp-store`, `trusted-schema` and
  `statement-cache`, the last from 0 to 1024 and 32 when absent;
- `interrupt-database!` may be called from any thread.

## 5. Order of work: M3's slices

Each slice is one PR, with its own mutation log, and ends with an Opus adversarial review. A check
isn't finished until it has been watched failing. Each slice's brief quotes the AGENTS.md rules
that bind what it builds.

0. **The plan and the records.** This plan; ADR-0016 to ADR-0020; the owed amendments (§10),
   applied on top of M2's merged records and re-read against them; the probes P1, P2, P3, P7, P9,
   P11, P12, P13 and P14, run on Guix's 3.53.1 and at the floor in
   [research/m3-plan/slice-0/](research/m3-plan/slice-0/README.md) (§8). Slice 0 lands after M2's
   slice 4, PR #19, and its closing review.
1. **The pure core,** on synthetic inputs, no foreign code:
   - the settings' plan: an options list and a settings list checked, each value's SQL text and
     its expected read-back, every refusal's rule (§4.3). Each domain is tested at its edges and
     against a value of every other kind, and the names against every setting SQLite has that the
     list leaves out (AGENTS.md: an allowlist against its whole complement). `statement-cache`
     among them: 0, 1, 32 and 1024 accepted; −1, 1025, `32.0`, `32.5`, `#t`,
     `"32"` and a symbol refused, each by its rule; and its absence giving 32;
   - the transaction's plan: from autocommit, depth and mode, the statements to issue, and from
     autocommit after the body, what to do on each exit (§4.2);

     (§4.1);

     over every primary code; `&sqlite-setting-mismatch`; `&sqlite-transaction-ended`;
   - the interrupt's exclusion, on stand-ins for `sqlite3_interrupt` and the close, across two
     threads, every wait bounded (§4.4);
   - a statement's first token in a script, past whitespace, comments and semicolons, by ADR-0014's
     scan, for a step error's offset (§4.1).
2. **The bindings.** The five entry points through `bind-required`; `SQLITE_PREPARE_PERSISTENT`

3. **The conveniences, the counters and the cache.**
   - **First, in M2's two statement libraries, as agreed with M2's session (finding F11):**

     their own names. **A `&who` row and a depth row at every raise site,** not one per verb, since
     a convenience raises from several internals:
     - for each of M2's verbs, its own raise;
     - for each convenience, a raise from each internal it reaches: the prepare, a bind, a step, a
       row read, the reset on return to the cache, and the finalize on eviction;
     - each names the procedure the caller called, and reaches a `with-exception-handler` handler at
       depth 1.
   - The private prepare with flags and with its tail (finding F1); reads and binds one value a
     section, through the who-taking internals (Q15).
   - `execute!`, `query`, `query-row`, `query-fold`, `execute-script!`, in §4.1's shapes. The
     re-entry rows:
